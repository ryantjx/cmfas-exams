# Appendix A — Review Questions

Candidates should note that the **sole purpose of the Review Questions is to familiarise candidates with the scope and general nature of the examinations, and the format of the examination questions**. They are **not intended as preparatory study material**, and **do not cover all the material tested**. The correct answer for each question is in **bold** in the source booklet and is reproduced as **Answer** below.

All **50** review questions appear here, in the booklet's own order and chapter grouping, so the appendix can be worked as a single timed set. The booklet prints the correct option in bold; that convention is preserved.

## Chapter 2 — Risk, Return and Time Value Calculations

**1.** As an investment manager, you are assessing a project with an immediate cash outflow followed by a series of cash inflows over the next 10 years. You calculate the project's net present value (NPV) — the sum of the present values of the cash inflows minus the initial cash outflow — and also its internal rate of return (IRR). You then discover you have **underestimated the discount rate**. What happens when you correct for the underestimation?

- **a.** **There will be a reduction to NPV with no change to IRR.**
- **b.** There will be no change to both NPV and IRR.
- **c.** There will be an increase to NPV and a reduction to IRR.
- **d.** Both NPV and IRR will increase.

**Answer: a.** NPV discounts the cash flows at the given rate, so raising the rate lowers NPV. IRR is the rate at which NPV equals zero and is a property of the cash flows alone, so it does not change.

**2.** A diversified portfolio has an expected return of **6%** and a standard deviation of **12%**. There is a **68%** probability of a return between **−6%** and **+18%** (plus or minus one standard deviation). Returns are normally distributed, so the probability of a loss exceeding 6% is **16%**. We can expect the portfolio's loss to exceed 6% once every ________.

- a. 5–6 years.
- **b.** **6–7 years.**
- c. 7–8 years.
- d. 8–9 years.

**Answer: b.** A 16% probability is about **1 in 6.25**, so roughly once every 6–7 years.

**3.** Which statements about value at risk (VaR) are TRUE?

- a. It is not an applicable measure for loss probability on assets such as bonds, shares and currencies.
- b. It measures and quantifies the minimum possible loss of an investment over a specific time and within a confidence level.
- c. It can be calculated by applying Monte Carlo simulation or the historical method.
- d. It can be used when determinants of future returns differ from those of past returns.

Options: (a) and (d) · (c) and (d) · (a) and (c) · **(b) and (c)**

**Answer: (b) and (c).** VaR quantifies the loss at a stated confidence level and can be computed historically or by Monte Carlo. It applies to bonds, shares and currencies, and its historical form assumes past determinants persist.

**4.** Which of the following are forms of operational risk?

- a. A flash crash caused by a massive erroneous sell order.
- b. Poor system infrastructure leading to technical disruptions.
- c. Human errors resulting in erroneous trades.
- d. Inability to access funding due to a credit downgrade of the borrower.

Options: **(a), (b) and (c)** · (b) and (c) · (b), (c) and (d) · (a) and (b)

**Answer: (a), (b) and (c).** Option (d) is **funding liquidity risk**, not operational risk.

## Chapter 3 — Key Drivers of Market Movements and Asset Values

**5.** Which of the following is a **leading** indicator?

- a. Average duration of unemployment.
- **b.** **The 10-year T-bond spread over the Fed Funds rate.**
- c. Nonfarm payroll employment.
- d. The average prime rate charged by banks.

**Answer: b.** The 10-year spread is one of the ten CLEI components. Average duration of unemployment and the average prime rate are **lagging**; nonfarm payrolls are **coincident**.

**6.** A central bank lowers the reserve requirement ratio. What is the most direct effect?

- a. The money supply contracts.
- **b.** **The money supply expands through credit creation.**
- c. Bond prices fall.
- d. The exchange rate appreciates immediately.

**Answer: b.** Lowering required reserves expands the money supply and multiplies lending through the banking system.

**7.** Which of the following best describes the **liquidity preference theory** of the yield curve?

- a. The curve's shape reflects investors' interest rate expectations.
- **b.** **The curve should always be upward sloping, because investors require a premium to hold longer maturities.**
- c. Each maturity's yield is set solely by supply and demand in that segment.
- d. The curve is flat when the market is ambivalent.

**Answer: b.** Liquidity preference holds that the curve is upward sloping because long-maturity securities are more price-volatile and require a higher return.

## Chapter 4 — Foreign Exchange

**8.** Spot USD/SGD is **1.2520** and the 30-day forward points are **−10**. The 30-day forward rate is:

- **a.** **1.2510**
- b. 1.2530
- c. 1.2520
- d. 1.2500

**Answer: a.** Negative points are subtracted: 1.2520 − 0.0010 = **1.2510**, a forward discount.

**9.** A Singapore investor holds a JPY portfolio that returned **121.88%** while SGD/JPY rose **18.89%**. The currency-adjusted return in SGD is approximately:

- a. 140.77%
- **b.** **86.62%**
- c. 102.99%
- d. 121.88%

**Answer: b.** The reference currency (SGD) is the **first** currency of the pair, so divide: (1 + 1.2188)/(1 + 0.1889) − 1 = **86.62%**.

**10.** Which of the following is a **non-deliverable forward** characteristic?

- a. Physical delivery of the domestic currency at maturity.
- b. Exchange of the full notional amount at settlement.
- **c.** **Cash settlement of the difference between the contracted rate and the spot rate, with no exchange of notional.**
- d. A tenor of five years or more.

**Answer: c.** An NDF is cash-settled with no delivery of the domestic currency and no exchange of notional; the normal tenor is **1 month to 1 year**.

## Chapter 5 — Company Analysis and Understanding Financial Statements

**11.** A company has current assets of **$500,000** and current liabilities of **$250,000**. Its current ratio is:

- a. 0.5
- **b.** **2.0**
- c. 1.5
- d. 2.5

**Answer: b.** 500,000 / 250,000 = **2.0**.

**12.** Which of the following would **improve** a company's reported ROE without any operational improvement?

- a. Increasing the dividend payout ratio.
- **b.** **Writing down goodwill against equity reserves.**
- c. Increasing inventory levels.
- d. Lengthening the depreciation period.

**Answer: b.** A goodwill write-down reduces equity (the denominator), raising ROE in later periods without any operational change.

**13.** Which of the following is a **non-cash** expense?

- a. Staff costs.
- b. Interest expense.
- **c.** **Depreciation.**
- d. Tax paid.

**Answer: c.** Depreciation is an accounting allocation, not a cash outflow, which is why it is added back in the cash flow statement.

**13a.** Derrick has prepared his company's financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2019, which reflect an operating profit of **$280,000**, current assets of **$510,000** and total assets of **$833,000**. He has since discovered that the depreciation figure he used was overstated, as he double counted the depreciation cost of **$55,000**. Which of the following statements is TRUE?

- **a.** **The revised operating profit for the year will be $335,000.**
- b. The revised depreciation expense figure will be $110,000.
- c. The revised current assets figure will be $565,000.
- d. The revised total assets figure will be $778,000.

**Answer: a.** Overstating depreciation by $55,000 understated operating profit by the same amount, so the correction adds it back: 280,000 + 55,000 = **$335,000**. Depreciation is a non-current asset adjustment, so current assets are unaffected — option (c) is wrong. Total assets rise by $55,000 to $888,000, not fall to $778,000.

**13b.** The purposes of the statement of cash flows are to:

- a. Evaluate liquidity.
- b. Facilitate banking relationships.
- c. Analyse cash position.
- d. Compare investment performance.

Options: (a) and (b) · **(a), (b) and (c)** · (a) and (c) · (a) and (d)

**Answer: (a), (b) and (c).** The statement of cash flows evaluates liquidity, facilitates banking relationships and analyses the cash position. Comparing investment performance is the job of return measurement, not the cash flow statement.

## Chapter 6 — Equity Securities

**14.** A company has **300 million** shares at **$2.00** and announces a **1-for-3** rights issue at **$1.90**. The theoretical ex-rights price is:

- a. $1.50
- **b.** **$1.975**
- c. $2.00
- d. $1.90

**Answer: b.** New shares 100m at $1.90 = $190m; total market value $600m + $190m = $790m over 400m shares = **$1.975**.

**15.** A stock trades at **$34.68** with a consensus next-year EPS of **$2.86**. Its prospective P/E is:

- a. 12.8x
- **b.** **12.1x**
- c. 11.7x
- d. 13.5x

**Answer: b.** 34.68 / 2.86 = **12.1x**.

**16.** Which index weighting scheme automatically adjusts for bonus and rights issues?

- **a.** **Value-weighted**
- b. Price-weighted
- c. Equal-weighted
- d. Geometric average

**Answer: a.** Capital changes do not affect market capitalisation, so a value-weighted index adjusts automatically. A price-weighted index needs a divisor adjustment.

**16a.** Which of the following statements are TRUE about preference shares?

- a. The preference rate of dividend is usually fixed and specified.
- b. The issuer is not obliged to pay the preference dividend if there are poor earnings.
- c. Preference shares can be issued as redeemable shares.
- d. The preference dividend must be paid after the ordinary dividend has been paid.

Options: **(a), (b) and (c)** · (b), (c) and (d) · (a), (c) and (d) · (a), (b), (c) and (d)

**Answer: (a), (b) and (c).** The preference dividend is usually a fixed, specified rate; the issuer is not obliged to pay it when earnings are poor, and a missed preference dividend is not a default; and preference shares may be issued as redeemable. Statement (d) reverses the priority — preference dividends rank **before** ordinary dividends.

**16b.** A price-weighted index consists of four stocks. One of the stocks is subject to a 3-for-1 stock split. What is most likely to happen to the divisor for the index?

- **a.** **Decrease.**
- b. Increase.
- c. Nullify.
- d. Remain unchanged.

**Answer: a.** A 3-for-1 split cuts that stock's price to a third. Because the index is the sum of prices divided by the divisor and the numerator has fallen, the divisor must be reduced to keep the index level unchanged.

**16c.** MaMa Rich is a listed food and beverage firm. Its stock is slightly more volatile than the market, with a beta of **1.3**, and the market's expected rate of return is **9.33%**. The US 5-year Treasury bill rate is currently **5.8%**. What is the firm's cost of equity capital?

- a. 9.515%
- b. 10.123%
- **c.** **10.389%**
- d. 11.761%

**Answer: c.** CAPM: R = Rf + β(Rm − Rf) = 5.8 + 1.3(9.33 − 5.8) = 5.8 + 1.3(3.53) = 5.8 + 4.589 = **10.389%**.

**16d.** The price-to-sales ratio (P/Sales) is a helpful valuation technique when examining firms ______________.

- a. That have negative earnings.
- b. In the technology sector only.
- c. That are highly leveraged.
- **d.** **In the start-up or early stage of their life cycle.**

**Answer: d.** P/Sales is useful where earnings are negative or unstable, which is typical of start-ups and early-stage firms. It is not confined to technology, and it does not correct for leverage — the ratio is conceptually flawed because the numerator relates to shareholders while sales are generated from funds supplied by both shareholders and debtholders.

**16e.** Which of the following are **not** advantages of American Depositary Receipts (ADRs)?

- a. They allow US investors to invest in foreign companies.
- b. They are denominated in US dollars and pay dividends in US dollars.
- c. They are traded on US exchanges.
- d. They eliminate currency risk.

Options: (a) and (b) · (b) and (c) · **(d) only** · (a), (b) and (c)

**Answer: (d) only.** ADRs give US investors access to foreign companies, are USD-denominated and pay USD dividends, and trade on US exchanges. They do **not** eliminate currency risk: the underlying shares remain denominated in the issuer's home currency, so the ADR price moves with the exchange rate.

## Chapter 7 — Deposits and Fixed-Income Securities

**17.** A T-bill with **90 days** to maturity, face value **$100,000**, sells for **$98,000**. The bank discount yield is:

- a. 2.04%
- **b.** **8.0%**
- c. 8.16%
- d. 7.35%

**Answer: b.** (2,000/100,000) × (360/90) = **8.0%**.

**18.** A **10-year** bond with par **$1,000** and an **8%** coupon, with a required yield of **6%** and annual coupons, is priced at approximately:

- a. $1,000.00
- **b.** **$1,147.20**
- c. $1,148.77
- d. $920.00

**Answer: b.** Discounting eight $80 coupons and the $1,000 principal at 6% gives **$1,147.20**. The semi-annual equivalent is $1,148.77.

**19.** Which bond has the **highest** convexity?

- a. A 5-year 8% coupon bond.
- b. A 10-year 8% coupon bond.
- c. A 10-year 4% coupon bond.
- **d.** **A 10-year zero-coupon bond.**

**Answer: d.** The lower the coupon, the higher the convexity; a zero-coupon bond has the highest convexity of all.

**20.** A convertible bond has a conversion ratio of **40**, a market price of **$1,100** and an underlying share price of **$24**. The conversion premium in percentage terms is:

- a. 4.2%
- **b.** **14.6%**
- c. 27.5%
- d. 51.0%

**Answer: b.** Conversion value = 40 × $24 = $960. Premium = ($1,100 − $960)/$960 = **14.6%**.

**20a.** As compared with bonds selling at par, deep discount bonds such as zero-coupon bonds will have:

- a. Greater reinvestment risk.
- **b.** **Greater price volatility.**
- c. Less call protection.
- d. None of the above.

**Answer: b.** A zero-coupon bond has **no** reinvestment risk if held to maturity, because there is no coupon to reinvest — so (a) is the opposite of the truth. Its price is the most sensitive to interest rate changes, because all of its cash flow arrives at maturity.

**20b.** What is the main feature of a callable bond?

- **a.** **It gives the issuer the option to redeem the bond before maturity.**
- b. It gives the buyer the option to redeem the bond before maturity.
- c. It gives the issuer the option to redeem the bond after maturity.
- d. It gives the buyer the option to redeem the bond after maturity.

**Answer: a.** The call option belongs to the **issuer**, not the buyer, and it is exercisable **before** maturity. The buyer's equivalent right is the put provision.

**20c.** Which of the following are potential risks that may arise when investing in foreign government bonds?

- a. Corporate sector downgrade risk.
- b. Sovereign credit risk.
- c. Currency risk.
- d. Political risk.

Options: (a), (b) and (c) · **(b), (c) and (d)** · (c) and (d) · (a) and (c)

**Answer: (b), (c) and (d).** A foreign government bond carries sovereign credit risk, currency risk and political risk. Corporate sector downgrade risk is not a feature of a sovereign issue.

**20d.** What is the value of a bond that matures in **3 years**, has an annual coupon of **$110**, and a par value of **$1,000**? Assume a required rate of return of **11%**.

- a. $330
- **b.** **$1,000**
- c. $970
- d. $1,330

**Answer: b.** The coupon rate is 110/1,000 = **11%**, exactly equal to the required return, so the bond prices at **par**. No discounting is needed once the coupon rate and the yield are seen to match.

**20e.** What happens to a bond's yield-to-maturity (YTM) if there is an increase in the bond price?

- a. The bond's YTM rises.
- **b.** **The bond's YTM falls.**
- c. The bond's YTM remains unchanged.
- d. The bond's YTM could fluctuate, depending on market conditions.

**Answer: b.** Price and yield are inversely related. A higher price means a lower yield to maturity.

## Chapter 8 — Portfolio Management

**21.** A portfolio has an expected return of **18%** and a standard deviation of **20%**; a second has **9%** and **10%**. With weights of **40%** and **60%** and a correlation of **0.8**, portfolio risk is:

- a. 14.00%
- b. 10.00%
- **c.** **13.30%**
- d. 2.00%

**Answer: c.** √(0.4²×0.2² + 0.6²×0.1² + 2×0.4×0.6×0.8×0.2×0.1) = √0.01768 = **13.30%**.

**22.** A portfolio returned **16%** with a standard deviation of **20%**; the risk-free rate is **6%**. Its Sharpe ratio is:

- a. 0.27
- **b.** **0.50**
- c. 0.39
- d. 0.80

**Answer: b.** (0.16 − 0.06)/0.20 = **0.50**.

**23.** Which measure is **most appropriate** for evaluating a portfolio manager?

- a. Dollar-weighted rate of return.
- **b.** **Time-weighted rate of return.**
- c. Current yield.
- d. Total return.

**Answer: b.** The TWR is unaffected by the size and timing of client cash flows, which the manager does not control.

**24.** A portfolio has a beta of **1.2**, the risk-free rate is **3%** and the market risk premium is **6%**. Its CAPM required return is:

- a. 7.2%
- b. 9.0%
- **c.** **10.2%**
- d. 12.0%

**Answer: c.** 3% + 1.2(6%) = **10.2%**.

**24a.** If you wish to eliminate the risk of your single-security portfolio, you will select a security that is ______________ to your portfolio.

- a. Perfectly positively correlated.
- **b.** **Perfectly negatively correlated.**
- c. Not correlated.
- d. Highly correlated.

**Answer: b.** Combining two securities with perfect negative correlation can eliminate portfolio risk altogether. This is the principle behind hedging strategies. Zero correlation reduces risk but does not eliminate it.

**24b.** Which of the following will affect the expected return of a portfolio under the Capital Asset Pricing Model?

- a. Risk-free rate.
- b. Idiosyncratic risk of an individual security.
- c. Systematic risk of the portfolio.
- d. Market risk premium.

Options: (a), (b) and (c) · (b), (c) and (d) · **(a), (c) and (d)** · (a), (b), (c) and (d)

**Answer: (a), (c) and (d).** CAPM prices only systematic risk, so idiosyncratic risk is not compensated — it can be diversified away. The risk-free rate, the portfolio's beta and the market risk premium are the three inputs.

**24c.** John recently bought a financial product with an initial investment capital of **$500,000**. At the end of the product's tenure his investment value is **$550,000**, and he received distributions worth **$10,000** in the interim. What was the return on John's investment?

- a. 2%
- b. 8%
- **c.** **12%**
- d. 10%

**Answer: c.** Total return includes distributions: (550,000 − 500,000 + 10,000)/500,000 = 60,000/500,000 = **12%**. Ignoring the distribution gives 10%, which is the trap.

**24d.** When analysing a portfolio with frequent injections of capital, what would be the best measure for evaluating the investment manager's performance?

- a. Simple return.
- b. Expected return.
- **c.** **Time-weighted return.**
- d. Dollar-weighted return.

**Answer: c.** The TWR removes the effect of the size and timing of client cash flows, which the manager does not control. The DWR is the IRR and is dominated by those flows.

**24e.** The Sharpe performance measure is defined as:

- a. The difference between the actual return on the portfolio and the return that would be expected from the capital asset pricing model.
- b. The portfolio's excess return divided by the portfolio's beta.
- c. The difference between the actual return on the portfolio and the return that would be expected from the capital asset pricing model, divided by the portfolio's total risk.
- **d.** **The portfolio's excess return divided by the portfolio's total risk.**

**Answer: d.** Sharpe = (Rp − Rf)/σp. Option (a) describes Jensen's alpha, and option (b) describes the Treynor ratio.

## Chapter 9 — ETFs, Unit Trusts, REITs and Insurance

**25.** Which of the following is **not** covered by the Singapore Deposit Insurance Scheme?

- a. A Singapore dollar savings account.
- b. A Singapore dollar fixed deposit.
- **c.** **A foreign currency deposit.**
- d. Monies under the CPF Investment Scheme.

**Answer: c.** The scheme covers **Singapore dollar-denominated** deposits only. Foreign currency deposits, structured deposits and investment products are excluded.

**26.** A REIT must invest at least what proportion of its fund in income-producing real estate?

- a. 45%
- b. 90%
- **c.** **75%**
- d. 50%

**Answer: c.** The Code on CIS requires a minimum of **75%** in income-producing real estate. The **90%** figure is the distribution requirement.

**27.** Which of the following is a feature of a **business trust** but not a REIT?

- a. A 45% gearing limit.
- b. A mandatory 90% distribution.
- **c.** **No gearing limit and no investment restrictions.**
- d. A separate trustee and manager.

**Answer: c.** Business trusts have **no gearing limit** and **no investment restrictions**, and use a single trustee-manager.

**28.** An investor pays **$1,000** a year for **5 years** at a **3%** discount rate. The present value is:

- a. $5,000.00
- **b.** **$4,579.71**
- c. $4,212.36
- d. $5,309.14

**Answer: b.** 1,000 × [1 − (1.03)⁻⁵]/0.03 = **$4,579.71**.

**28a.** Which of the following are advantages of investing in ETFs?

- a. Access to a superior hedge fund manager.
- b. Small capital outlay.
- c. Low costs.
- d. Transparency.

Options: (a), (b) and (d) · (b) and (c) · (a), (c) and (d) · **(b), (c) and (d)**

**Answer: (b), (c) and (d).** ETFs offer a small capital outlay, low costs and transparency. They give no access to a hedge fund manager, which is a feature of a fund of hedge funds.

**28b.** Which of the following is **not** a feature of real estate investment?

- a. Provides security of income.
- b. Provides a hedge against inflation.
- **c.** **Requires a high capital outlay.**
- d. Requires annual injection of capital.

**Answer: c.** A high capital outlay is a genuine feature of direct real estate investment, so it cannot be the exception. The statement that does not hold is that real estate requires an annual injection of capital: the asset is held, not topped up each year.

**28c.** Which of the following statements are CORRECT?

- a. Apart from REITs, trusts that invest in real estate can be structured as business trusts.
- b. A business trust has a single responsible entity that serves as both the trustee and the manager.
- c. A business trust has no gearing limit.
- d. Both REITs and business trusts are required to distribute a minimum of 90% of annual income.

Options: (a), (c) and (d) · **(a), (b) and (c)** · (b), (c) and (d) · (a), (b), (c) and (d)

**Answer: (a), (b) and (c).** Statement (d) is wrong: only a REIT carries the 90% distribution requirement. A business trust has no mandatory minimum.

**28d.** Which of the following are main characteristics of hedge funds?

- a. Use of leverage to increase potential returns.
- b. Measured against absolute returns rather than relative returns.
- c. Lower fees than other financial products.
- d. Relatively unrestricted investment style.

Options: **(a), (b) and (d)** · (b), (c) and (d) · (a), (b) and (c) · (a), (c) and (d)

**Answer: (a), (b) and (d).** Hedge funds use leverage, target absolute returns and are relatively unrestricted. Their fees are **higher**, not lower, than those of conventional funds — typically a management fee plus a performance fee.

**28e.** Which statement best describes private equity investments?

- a. Investments that are exclusive to private banking clients.
- **b.** **Investments in companies that are not publicly listed.**
- c. Investments in companies that operate in the private sector.
- d. Investments that are not publicly disclosed to outsiders and non-clients.

**Answer: b.** Private equity is investment in companies that are not publicly listed. The private sector is not the defining test — a listed company is in the private sector too — and private equity is not restricted to private banking clients.

## Chapter 10 — Warrants

**29.** A warrant has a share price of **$2.50**, an exercise price of **$1.50** and a conversion ratio of **1**. Its intrinsic value is:

- a. $0
- **b.** **$1.00**
- c. $1.50
- d. $2.50

**Answer: b.** (2.50 − 1.50)/1 = **$1.00**.

**30.** A warrant trades at **$1.30** with a share price of **$2.50** and an exercise price of **$1.50**, conversion ratio 1. Its gearing ratio is approximately:

- a. 1.9x
- **b.** **1.9x to 2.0x depending on the share price used**
- c. 8.8x
- d. 0.5x

**Answer: b.** Gearing = SP/(n × WP) = 2.50/1.30 = **1.92x**.

**31.** Which factor **reduces** a company warrant's price?

- a. An increase in the underlying share price.
- b. An increase in volatility.
- **c.** **An increase in dividend expectations.**
- d. An increase in the remaining life of the warrant.

**Answer: c.** Warrant holders do not receive dividends, so higher dividend expectations reduce the call warrant's price.

**31a.** Company A's warrant is trading at **$0.35**; its exercise price is **$0.50** while the share price is **$0.65**. What is its intrinsic value?

- a. $0.85
- **b.** **$0.15**
- c. $0.30
- d. $1.00

**Answer: b.** Intrinsic value = (SP − EP)/n = (0.65 − 0.50)/1 = **$0.15**.

**31b.** A warrant with an exercise price of **$1.00** is trading at **$1.20**. One warrant converts to one share. What is its conversion price?

- a. $1.00
- b. $1.20
- **c.** **$2.20**
- d. $0.20

**Answer: c.** Conversion price = n × WP + EP = 1 × 1.20 + 1.00 = **$2.20**. This is the total the investor pays to convert warrants into one share.

**31c.** The market price of Company B's common share is **$5** and the exercise price of the warrant is **$3.50**. Two warrants convert into one common share. The warrant price is **$2**. What is the warrant premium?

- a. 10%
- **b.** **50%**
- c. 57%
- d. 100%

**Answer: b.** Premium ($) = nWP + EP − SP = 2(2.00) + 3.50 − 5.00 = **$2.50**. Premium (%) = 2.50/5.00 × 100 = **50%**. The conversion ratio of 2 is the trap: using n = 1 gives (2.00 + 3.50 − 5.00)/5.00 = 10%, which is option (a).

**31d.** A warrant is trading at **$0.40**. The exercise price is **$0.80**. The share price is **$2.40**. What is its gearing? Assume the warrant is company-issued, so the conversion ratio is 1.

- a. 2x
- **b.** **6x**
- c. 3x
- d. 5x

**Answer: b.** Gearing = SP/(nWP) = 2.40/0.40 = **6x**.

**31e.** Which of the following will directly affect the price of a company warrant?

- a. The length of time before the expiration date.
- b. The coupon rate of bonds issued by the company.
- c. The underlying share price.
- d. The number of warrants issued by the company.

Options: (a), (b) and (c) · **(a), (c) and (d)** · (b) and (d) · (a) and (c)

**Answer: (a), (c) and (d).** Time to expiry, the underlying share price and the size of the warrant issue all affect the warrant price directly. The coupon rate on the company's bonds is not a direct determinant.

## Chapter 11 — Technical Analysis and Quantitative Analysis

**32.** An RSI reading of **75** most likely indicates:

- a. An oversold condition.
- **b.** **An overbought condition.**
- c. A neutral market.
- d. A trend reversal has already occurred.

**Answer: b.** An RSI above **70** is considered overbought; below **30** is oversold.

**33.** Which chart pattern is a **reversal** pattern?

- a. Ascending triangle.
- b. Flag.
- **c.** **Head and shoulders.**
- d. Consolidation rectangle.

**Answer: c.** Head and shoulders is a reversal pattern. Triangles, flags and rectangles are continuation patterns.

**34.** Under the Dow Theory, the secondary movement typically retraces what proportion of the previous advance?

- a. One-tenth to one-quarter.
- **b.** **One-third to two-thirds.**
- c. Three-quarters to nine-tenths.
- d. The full amount.

**Answer: b.** The secondary movement usually retraces **one-third to two-thirds** of the previous advance or decline.

**34a.** The technical analyst is one who:

- a. Buys shares whose intrinsic value is higher than the market price.
- b. Tends to be long-term oriented.
- c. Studies the prospects of earnings growth in forecasting future price trends.
- **d.** **Studies price and volume patterns of stocks.**

**Answer: d.** Technical analysis studies historical market data — price changes and trading volume. Options (a) and (c) describe fundamental analysis, and technical analysts are typically shorter-term oriented than fundamental analysts.

**34b.** Which of the following indicates a buy signal to technical analysts?

- **a.** **The stock breaks through the moving average line from below.**
- b. A head and shoulders pattern is formed with a breakout below the neckline by 3% of the share price.
- c. Both the Dow Jones Industrial Average and the Dow Jones Transportation Average are moving down.
- d. The advance-decline line is falling in a rising market.

**Answer: a.** A break upward through the moving average is a bullish crossover. A head and shoulders breakout below the neckline is a sell signal, both averages moving down confirms a downtrend, and a falling advance-decline line in a rising market signals a market peak.

**34c.** Which of the following are bullish indicators?

- a. Ascending triangles.
- b. Descending triangles.
- c. Double bottoms.
- d. Reverse head and shoulders.

Options: **(a), (c) and (d)** · (b), (c) and (d) · (b) and (c) · (c) and (d)

**Answer: (a), (c) and (d).** Ascending triangles, double bottoms and inverse (reverse) head and shoulders are all bullish. A descending triangle is a bearish indicator.

**34d.** According to the Dow Theory, daily fluctuations are considered:

- a. The secondary trend.
- b. A moving average.
- c. The primary trend.
- **d.** **Random and have no long-term forecasting value.**

**Answer: d.** The Dow Theory holds that daily fluctuations are random noise with no forecasting value. Only the primary and secondary movements carry information.

**34e.** A low relative strength indicator (RSI) reading, below 20, could indicate that the stock is:

- a. At a market peak and likely to head down.
- b. Overbought.
- **c.** **Oversold.**
- d. Performing well compared with other stocks in the same risk category.

**Answer: c.** An RSI below 30 signals an oversold condition; below 20 is deeply oversold. Above 70 is overbought.

## Chapter 12 — Case Studies

**35.** An individual earns **S$400,000** a year. Under the SFA, is the individual an accredited investor?

- **a.** **Yes, because income in the preceding 12 months is at least S$300,000.**
- b. No, because net personal assets are not stated.

**Answer: a.** Any one of the three individual tests suffices; income of at least **S$300,000** in the preceding 12 months qualifies.

**36.** A carry trade borrows in a **1.5%** currency and invests in a **6%** currency, with the exchange rate unchanged. On an SGD-equivalent exposure of **SGD 9,923,903.51**, the annual gain is:

- a. SGD 148,858.55
- **b.** **SGD 446,575.66**
- c. SGD 595,434.21
- d. SGD 9,923,903.51

**Answer: b.** (0.06 − 0.015) × 9,923,903.51 = **SGD 446,575.66**.

**37.** A stock trades at **$40** with next-year EPS of **$1.08** and a 5-year EPS growth rate of **38.99%**. Its PEG ratio is approximately:

- a. 0.92
- **b.** **0.95**
- c. 1.05
- d. 1.37

**Answer: b.** Prospective P/E = 40/1.08 = 37.04; PEG = 37.04/38.99 = **0.95**.

## Exam cues

- **NPV falls when the discount rate rises; IRR does not change** — IRR is a property of the cash flows alone.
- **±1 standard deviation = 68%; ±2 = 95.4%.** A 16% tail probability is about **1 in 6.25**.
- **VaR** can be computed historically or by Monte Carlo; it applies to bonds, shares and currencies.
- **Funding liquidity risk is not operational risk.**
- **Leading:** 10-year T-bond spread over Fed Funds. **Coincident:** nonfarm payrolls. **Lagging:** average duration of unemployment, average prime rate.
- **Lower reserve requirements expand the money supply** through credit creation.
- **Liquidity preference:** the curve is always upward sloping because long maturities need a premium.
- **Forward points:** negative → subtract; positive → add. 1.2520 with −10 points = **1.2510**.
- **Currency adjustment:** reference currency first in the pair → **divide**; second → **multiply**.
- **NDF:** cash-settled, no delivery, no notional exchange, tenor **1 month to 1 year**.
- **Current ratio** = current assets / current liabilities.
- **Goodwill write-downs raise ROE** by shrinking the equity denominator.
- **Depreciation is non-cash** and is added back in the cash flow statement.
- **Rights issue:** theoretical ex-rights price = (old market value + new capital) / new share count.
- **Prospective P/E** = price / next-year EPS.
- **Value-weighted indices adjust automatically** for capital changes.
- **Bank discount yield** = (D/F) × (360/t).
- **Bond price** falls as the required yield rises above the coupon.
- **Zero-coupon bonds have the highest convexity.**
- **Conversion premium (%)** = (market value of CB − conversion value) / conversion value.
- **Portfolio risk is not a weighted average** of constituent risks.
- **Sharpe** = excess return / standard deviation.
- **TWR is the correct measure for judging a manager.**
- **CAPM** = $$R_f + \beta(R_m - R_f)$$.
- **Deposit insurance covers S$ deposits only.**
- **REIT: 75% investment test, 90% distribution test, 45% gearing cap.**
- **Business trust: no gearing cap, no investment restrictions, single trustee-manager.**
- **Annuity PV** = $$C[1 - (1+i)^{-n}]/i$$.
- **Warrant intrinsic value** = (SP − EP)/n; **gearing** = SP/(n × WP).
- **Dividends reduce a call warrant's price.**
- **RSI above 70 overbought, below 30 oversold.**
- **Head and shoulders is a reversal pattern.**
- **Dow Theory secondary movement retraces one-third to two-thirds.**
- **Accredited investor: any one of the three individual tests suffices.**
- **Carry trade gain** = interest differential × SGD-equivalent exposure when the rate is flat.
- **PEG below 1 suggests undervaluation.**
