# Appendix B — Formulae Sheet

This formulae sheet is provided during the examination. The examination might not cover all formulas provided. Every symbol is defined where it first appears.

## Risk, Return and Time Value Calculations

### Measures of return

$$\text{Total Return on a Security} = \frac{\text{Income} + \text{Capital Gain or Loss}}{\text{Purchase Price}}$$

$$\text{Expected Rate of Return} = E(R) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} r_i p_i$$

where $$E(R)$$ is the expected return on a security, $$r_i$$ the $$i$$th possible return, $$p_i$$ the probability of that return, and $$n$$ the number of possible returns.

### Measures of risk

$$\text{Variance of Returns} = \sigma^2 = \sum p_i \left[ r_i - E(R) \right]^2$$

$$\text{Standard Deviation of Returns} = \sigma = \sqrt{\sigma^2}$$

$$\text{Coefficient of Variation} = CV = \frac{\text{Standard Deviation of Returns}}{\text{Expected Rate of Return}}$$

### Time value of money

$$\text{Simple Interest} = \frac{\text{Principal} \times \text{Interest Rate (in \%)} \times \text{Interest Period (in days)}}{36{,}500}$$

$$FV_n = PV(1 + k)^n$$

where $$FV_n$$ is the future value at the end of $$n$$ years, $$PV$$ the present value or original amount invested at the beginning of the first year, $$n$$ the number of years of compounding, and $$k$$ the annual interest rate.

$$CAGR = \left[ \frac{FV}{PV} \right]^{1/n} - 1$$

where $$CAGR$$ is the compounded annual growth rate, $$FV$$ the future value, $$PV$$ the present value, and $$n$$ the number of years.

$$FV_n = PV\left(1 + \frac{k}{m}\right)^{mn}$$

where $$m$$ is the number of interest periods in the year.

### Present value of a single cash flow

$$PV = \frac{FV_n}{(1 + k)^n}$$

where $$PV$$ is the present value of the future sum, $$FV_n$$ the future value at the end of $$n$$ years, $$n$$ the number of years to receipt, and $$k$$ the annual discount or interest rate.

## Key Drivers of Market Movements and Asset Values

### Quantity theory of money

$$M \times V = P \times Q$$

where $$M$$ is the stock of money (currency outside banks plus demand deposits), $$V$$ the velocity of money circulation (the number of times per period an average unit of money changes hands), $$P$$ the general price level or price index, and $$Q$$ the number of transactions in an economy during a year.

## Foreign Exchange

### Forward foreign exchange contract

$$\text{Forward Points} = \frac{S \times (R_c - R_b) \times n/360}{1 + (R_b \times n/360)}$$

where $$S$$ is the spot rate, $$R_c$$ the annualised interest rate of the counter currency, $$R_b$$ the annualised interest rate of the base currency, and $$n$$ the number of days from the spot date.

### Interest rate parity

$$F = S \left[ \frac{1 + R_c \left( \frac{n}{360} \right)}{1 + R_b \left( \frac{n}{360} \right)} \right]$$

where $$F$$ is the forward rate, $$S$$ the spot rate, $$R_c$$ the annualised counter-currency rate, $$R_b$$ the annualised base-currency rate, and $$n$$ the number of days.

## Company Analysis and Understanding Financial Statements

### Per share ratios

$$\text{Book Value Per Share} = \frac{\text{Shareholders' Equity}}{\text{Total Number of Shares Outstanding}}$$

$$\text{Fully Diluted Earnings Per Share} = \frac{\text{Net Earnings}}{\text{Fully-Diluted Number of Shares}}$$

$$\text{Dividend Per Share} = \frac{\text{Dividends Paid}}{\text{Total Number of Shares Outstanding}}$$

### Liquidity ratios

$$\text{Current Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$$

$$\text{Quick Ratio} = \frac{\text{Cash} + \text{Marketable Securities} + \text{Accounts Receivable}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$$

$$\text{Cash Ratio} = \frac{\text{Cash} + \text{Near-Cash}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$$

$$\text{Cash Flow Ratio} = \frac{\text{Cash Flow From Operating Activities}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}$$

### Leverage ratios

$$\text{Debt/Equity Ratio} = \frac{\text{Total Debt}}{\text{Total Shareholders' Equity}}$$

$$\text{Debt-to-Total Assets Ratio} = \frac{\text{Total Debt}}{\text{Total Assets}}$$

$$\text{Total Liabilities-to-Total Assets Ratio} = \frac{\text{Total Liabilities}}{\text{Total Assets}}$$

$$\text{Interest Coverage} = \frac{\text{Earnings Before Interest and Taxes}}{\text{Interest Expense}}$$

$$\text{EBITDA Interest Coverage} = \frac{\text{EBITDA}}{\text{Interest Expense}}$$

$$\text{Total Fixed Charge Coverage} = \frac{\text{Earnings Before Interest, Taxes and Lease Payments}}{\text{Interest Expense} + \text{Lease Payments} + \dfrac{\text{Preferred Dividend}}{1 - \text{Tax Rate}}}$$

### Operating efficiency / activity ratios

$$\text{Total Asset Turnover} = \frac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Average Total Assets}}$$

$$\text{Inventory Turnover} = \frac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Average Inventory}}$$

$$\text{Days to Sell Inventory} = \frac{365 \text{ days}}{\text{Inventory Turnover}} = \frac{\text{Average Inventory}}{\text{Sales}/365}$$

$$\text{Receivables Turnover} = \frac{\text{Sales}}{\text{Average Accounts Receivables}}$$

$$\text{Collection Period} = \frac{365}{\text{Receivables Turnover}} = \frac{\text{Average Accounts Receivables}}{\text{Sales}/365}$$

### Profitability ratios

$$\text{Gross Profit Margin} = \frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Sales}}$$

$$\text{Operating Profit Margin} = \frac{\text{Earnings Before Interest and Tax}}{\text{Sales}}$$

$$\text{Net Profit Margin} = \frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{Sales}}$$

$$\text{Return on Equity} = \frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{Average Common Equity}}$$

$$\text{Pre-tax Return on Assets} = \frac{\text{Pre-Tax Income} + \text{Interest Expense}}{\text{Average Total Assets}}$$

$$\text{After-tax Return on Assets} = \frac{\text{Net Income} + \text{Interest Expense}(1 - \text{Tax Rate})}{\text{Average Total Assets}}$$

$$\text{Earnings Per Share} = \frac{\text{Net Income Available to Common Shareholders}}{\text{Average Number of Common Shares Outstanding}}$$

## Equity Securities

### Revised net asset value per share

$$RNAVPS = \frac{\text{Revised Asset Value} - \text{Market Value of Liabilities}}{\text{Total Outstanding Number of Shares}}$$

### Valuation ratios

$$\text{Price-Earnings Ratio (P/E)} = \frac{\text{Market Price Per Share}}{\text{Earnings Per Share}}$$

$$\text{Price-Earnings Growth Ratio (PEG)} = \frac{P/E}{\% \text{ Growth in Earnings}}$$

$$\text{Price-to-Book Value Ratio (P/BV)} = \frac{\text{Market Price Per Share}}{\text{Book Value Per Share}}$$

$$\text{Price-to-Revised Net Asset Value Ratio (P/RNAV)} = \frac{\text{Market Price Per Share}}{(\text{Market Value of Assets} - \text{Liabilities})/\text{Shares Outstanding}}$$

$$\text{Price-to-Sales Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Market Price Per Share}}{\text{Sales Per Share}}$$

$$\text{Enterprise Value-to-Cash Flow Ratio} = \frac{\text{Market Cap} + \text{Debt} + \text{Preferred Shares} - \text{Cash}}{\text{Cash Flow from Operations}}$$

$$\text{Enterprise Value-to-EBITDA Ratio} = \frac{\text{Market Cap} + \text{Debt} + \text{Preferred} + \text{Minority Interest} + \text{Unfunded Pension} - \text{Associates} - \text{Cash \& Near Cash}}{\text{Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation and Amortisation}}$$

### Dividend yield

$$\text{Dividend Yield} = \frac{\text{Dividend Per Share}}{\text{Market Price Per Share}} \times 100$$

### Dividend discount model

$$P_0 = \frac{D_1}{(1+k)} + \frac{D_2}{(1+k)^2} + \frac{D_3}{(1+k)^3} + \dots + \frac{D_\infty}{(1+k)^\infty}$$

where $$P_0$$ is the intrinsic or theoretical value of the share today, $$D_t$$ the dividends expected at the end of period $$t$$, and $$k$$ the required rate of return.

With a subsequent sale of the share:

$$P_0 = \frac{D_1}{(1+k)} + \frac{D_2}{(1+k)^2} + \dots + \frac{D_n}{(1+k)^n} + \frac{P_n}{(1+k)^n}$$

where $$n$$ is the holding period and $$P_n$$ the expected selling price at the end of period $$n$$.

### Constant growth dividend discount model

$$P_0 = \frac{D_0(1+g)}{(1+k)} + \frac{D_0(1+g)^2}{(1+k)^2} + \frac{D_0(1+g)^3}{(1+k)^3} + \dots + \frac{D_0(1+g)^\infty}{(1+k)^\infty}$$

where $$D_0$$ is the current dividend and $$g$$ the constant growth rate. This simplifies to:

$$P_0 = \frac{D_1}{k - g}$$

where $$D_1$$ is the dividend expected at the end of period 1.

$$\text{Required Rate of Return } (k) = \frac{D_1}{P_0} + g$$

$$g = \text{Retention Rate} \times \text{Return on Equity} = RR \times ROE$$

### Zero-growth model

$$P_0 = \frac{\text{Dividend}}{\text{Discount Rate}}$$

### Multi-stage growth dividend discount model

For two-stage dividend growth:

$$P_0 = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{D_0(1+g_1)^t}{(1+k)^t} + \frac{\dfrac{D_n(1+g_c)}{k - g_c}}{(1+k)^n}$$

where $$P_0$$ is the intrinsic value today, $$D_0$$ the current dividend, $$g_1$$ the growth rate when dividends are more predictable, $$g_c$$ the constant growth rate when dividends are less predictable, $$k$$ the required rate of return, $$n$$ the number of periods of supernormal or subnormal growth, and $$D_n$$ the dividend at the end of the abnormal growth period.

## Deposits and Fixed-Income Securities

### T-bill yield

$$Y_d = \frac{D}{F} \times \frac{360}{t}$$

where $$Y_d$$ is the annualised yield on a bank discount basis (as a decimal), $$D$$ the dollar discount (face value minus price), $$F$$ the face value, and $$t$$ the number of days remaining to maturity.

### Bond valuation

With annual interest:

$$P = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{C_t}{(1+k)^t} + \frac{M}{(1+k)^n}$$

where $$P$$ is the present value of the bond today, $$C_t$$ the coupon payments for period $$t$$, $$M$$ the maturity value, $$n$$ the number of years to maturity, and $$k$$ the required rate of return.

With semi-annual interest:

$$P = \sum_{t=1}^{2n} \frac{C_t}{(1 + k/2)^t} + \frac{M}{(1 + k/2)^{2n}}$$

Zero-coupon bond:

$$P = \frac{M}{(1 + k/2)^{2n}}$$

### Yield measures

$$\text{Current Yield} = \frac{\text{Annual Interest Payment}}{\text{Bond Price}}$$

Yield-to-maturity $$k$$ solves:

$$P = \frac{C_1}{1 + k/2} + \frac{C_2}{(1 + k/2)^2} + \frac{C_3}{(1 + k/2)^3} + \dots + \frac{C_{2n}}{(1 + k/2)^{2n}} + \frac{M}{(1 + k/2)^{2n}}$$

$$\text{Effective Annual Yield} = (1 + \text{Semi-Annual Interest Rate})^2 - 1$$

$$\text{Approximate Yield-to-Maturity} = \frac{\text{Annual Interest Payment} \pm \text{Amortised Gain or Loss}}{(\text{Current Market Price} + \text{Par Value})/2}$$

Yield-to-maturity for a zero-coupon bond:

$$\frac{k}{2} = \left[ \frac{\text{Maturity Value}}{\text{Price}} \right]^{1/2n} - 1$$

$$\text{Taxable Equivalent Yield} = \frac{\text{Tax Exempt Yield}}{1 - \text{Marginal Tax Rate}}$$

### Analysis of convertible bonds

$$\text{Conversion Value} = \text{Conversion Ratio} \times \text{Share Price}$$

$$\text{Conversion Ratio} = \frac{\text{Par Value of Convertible Bond}}{\text{Conversion Price}}$$

$$\text{Conversion Parity Price} = \frac{\text{Market Price of Convertible Bond}}{\text{Conversion Ratio}}$$

$$\text{Conversion Premium (\$)} = \text{Market Value of Convertible Bond} - \text{Conversion Value}$$

$$\text{Conversion Premium (\%)} = \frac{\text{Market Value of Convertible Bond} - \text{Conversion Value}}{\text{Conversion Value}}$$

$$\text{Breakeven Time/Payback} = \frac{\text{Conversion Premium}}{\text{Bond Income} - \text{Dividend Income}}$$

$$\text{Premium Over Straight Value (\%)} = \frac{\text{Market Price of Convertible Bond}}{\text{Investment Value}} - 1$$

## Portfolio Management

### Calculating portfolio return and risk

$$\text{Expected Return of a Portfolio} = R_p = \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_i r_i$$

where $$R_p$$ is the expected portfolio return, $$w_i$$ the portfolio weight for security $$i$$, $$r_i$$ the expected return on security $$i$$, $$n$$ the number of securities, and $$\sum w_i = 1.0$$.

$$\text{Portfolio Risk (two assets)} = \sigma_p = \sqrt{w_1^2 \sigma_1^2 + w_2^2 \sigma_2^2 + 2 w_1 w_2 \rho_{1,2} \sigma_1 \sigma_2}$$

where $$w_1$$ and $$w_2$$ are the portfolio weights, $$\sigma_1$$ and $$\sigma_2$$ the standard deviations, and $$\rho_{1,2}$$ the correlation coefficient.

### Capital market line

$$E(R_p) = R_f + \frac{E(R_m) - R_f}{\sigma_m} \sigma_p$$

where $$E(R_p)$$ is the expected return on any efficient portfolio P on the CML, $$R_f$$ the risk-free rate, $$E(R_m)$$ the expected return on the market portfolio, $$\sigma_m$$ the standard deviation of market returns, and $$\sigma_p$$ the standard deviation of portfolio P's returns.

### Capital asset pricing model

$$E(R_i) = R_f + \beta_i (R_m - R_f)$$

where $$E(R_i)$$ is the expected return on security or portfolio $$i$$, $$R_f$$ the risk-free rate, $$R_m$$ the expected market return, $$\beta_i$$ the systematic risk relative to the market, and $$R_m - R_f$$ the market risk premium.

### Arbitrage pricing theory

$$R_i = E(R_i) + b_{i1}f_1 + b_{i2}f_2 + \dots + b_{in}f_n + e_i$$

where $$R_i$$ is the actual return on security $$i$$ in a period, $$E(R_i)$$ the expected return, $$f$$ the deviation of a systematic factor from its expected value, $$b_i$$ the sensitivity of security $$i$$'s returns to a factor, and $$e_i$$ a random error term unique to security $$i$$.

### Measuring investment returns

No deposits or withdrawals:

$$R_p = \frac{V_E - V_B}{V_B}$$

where $$V_E$$ is the end value and $$V_B$$ the beginning value.

Deposits or withdrawals at the **end** of the measurement period:

$$R_p = \frac{(V_E + \text{Withdrawals} - \text{Deposits}) - V_B}{V_B}$$

Deposits or withdrawals at the **beginning** of the measurement period:

$$R_p = \frac{V_E - (V_B + \text{Deposits} - \text{Withdrawals})}{V_B + \text{Deposits} - \text{Withdrawals}}$$

Annualising returns:

$$\text{Annual Return} = [(1 + r_1)(1 + r_2)(1 + r_3)(1 + r_4)] - 1$$

where $$r_1, r_2, r_3, r_4$$ are the quarterly returns.

Modified Dietz method:

$$R_p = \frac{V_E - V_B - CF}{V_B + WCF}$$

where $$CF$$ is the net cash flow over the period and $$WCF$$ the weighted cash flow:

$$WCF = \sum_i CF_i \frac{n - t_i}{n}$$

where $$CF_i$$ is the cash flow on day $$t_i$$, $$t_i$$ the number of days from the beginning of the period until $$CF_i$$, and $$n$$ the number of days in the period.

### Risk-adjusted measures of performance

$$\text{Sharpe ratio} = \frac{R_p - R_f}{\sigma_p}$$

where $$R_p$$ is the average portfolio return, $$R_f$$ the risk-free rate, and $$\sigma_p$$ the standard deviation of portfolio returns.

$$\text{Treynor ratio} = \frac{R_p - R_f}{\beta_p}$$

where $$\beta_p$$ is the portfolio beta.

Jensen's alpha. The CAPM expected return is:

$$R_e = R_f + \beta_p (R_m - R_f)$$

The portfolio's alpha is the difference between the actual return $$R_a$$ and the CAPM-expected return:

$$\alpha_p = R_a - R_e = R_a - [R_f + \beta_p(R_m - R_f)]$$

$$\text{Information ratio} = \frac{\alpha_p}{\sigma_p}$$

where $$\alpha_p$$ is Jensen's alpha and $$\sigma_p$$ the excess risk.

$$\text{Sortino ratio} = \frac{R_p - R_f}{\sigma_{\text{negative}}}$$

where $$\sigma_{\text{negative}}$$ is the average downside volatility.

## ETFs, Unit Trusts, REITs and Insurance

### Net asset value

$$\text{NAV} = \frac{\text{Fund Securities} + \text{Other Fund Assets} - \text{Liabilities (including management fees)} - \text{Debt used for leveraging}}{\text{Total fund units outstanding}}$$

### Annuity calculations

$$\text{Present value, } PV_{\text{annuity}} = C \times \left[ \frac{1 - (1+i)^{-n}}{i} \right]$$

$$\text{Future value, } FV_{\text{annuity}} = C \times \left[ \frac{(1+i)^n - 1}{i} \right]$$

where $$C$$ is the cash flow per period, $$i$$ the interest rate, and $$n$$ the number of payments.

## Warrants

### Warrant valuation

$$\text{Intrinsic Value} = \frac{SP - EP}{n}$$

where $$SP$$ is the share price, $$EP$$ the exercise price, and $$n$$ the conversion ratio.

$$\text{Conversion Price} = n \times WP + EP$$

where $$WP$$ is the warrant price.

$$\text{Warrant Premium (\$)} = n \times WP + EP - SP$$

$$\text{Warrant Premium (\%)} = \frac{n \times WP + EP - SP}{SP} \times 100$$

$$\text{Gearing Ratio} = \frac{SP}{n \times WP}$$

$$\text{Minimum Value of Warrant} = SP - EP \text{ if } SP > EP; \qquad = 0 \text{ if } SP \le EP$$

## Exam cues

- **Six return measures:** total, expected, annualised, real, currency-adjusted, IRR. Only total and expected appear on this sheet.
- **Risk measures:** variance, standard deviation, coefficient of variation. CV is the only one expressed per unit of return.
- **TVM:** $$FV_n = PV(1+k)^n$$; $$FV_n = PV(1 + k/m)^{mn}$$; $$PV = FV_n/(1+k)^n$$; $$CAGR = (FV/PV)^{1/n} - 1$$. Simple interest uses **36,500** as the denominator.
- **Fisher's equation of exchange:** $$M \times V = P \times Q$$.
- **Forward points:** $$S(R_c - R_b)(n/360) / [1 + R_b(n/360)]$$. **Interest rate parity:** $$F = S[(1 + R_c n/360)/(1 + R_b n/360)]$$.
- **Per-share denominators differ:** BVPS uses **total shares outstanding**; diluted EPS uses **fully diluted shares**; DPS uses **total shares outstanding**.
- **Liquidity ratios:** current, quick (excludes inventory), cash, cash flow.
- **Leverage ratios:** debt/equity, debt/total assets, total liabilities/total assets, interest coverage, EBITDA interest coverage, total fixed charge coverage (preferred dividend grossed up by dividing by 1 − tax rate).
- **Efficiency ratios use AVERAGE balances** in the denominator.
- **Profitability:** gross, operating, net margins; ROE; pre-tax and after-tax ROA; EPS.
- **Valuation ratios:** P/E, PEG, P/BV, P/RNAV, P/Sales, EV/CF, EV/EBITDA. **EV/EBITDA adds minorities and unfunded pension and subtracts associates and cash.**
- **DDM family:** general DDM, DDM with a subsequent sale, constant growth (Gordon), zero growth, two-stage. **$$g = RR \times ROE$$.**
- **T-bill yield:** $$(D/F)(360/t)$$.
- **Bond pricing:** annual, semi-annual (double the periods, halve the rate), zero-coupon.
- **Yield measures:** current yield, YTM, effective annual yield, approximate YTM, zero-coupon YTM, taxable equivalent yield.
- **Convertible measures:** conversion value, conversion ratio, conversion parity price, conversion premium in dollars and per cent, breakeven time, premium over straight value.
- **Portfolio risk** uses the two-asset formula with the correlation term; **portfolio return** is a simple weighted average.
- **CML uses total risk (σ); CAPM/SML uses beta.**
- **APT** is the multifactor model.
- **Return measurement:** no cash flow, cash flow at end, cash flow at start, annualising, modified Dietz with weighted cash flow.
- **Risk-adjusted measures:** Sharpe (σ), Treynor (β), Jensen's alpha, information ratio, Sortino (downside σ).
- **NAV** subtracts liabilities including management fees and any debt used for leveraging.
- **Annuity PV and FV** use the same bracket structure with the sign of the exponent reversed.
- **Warrant formulas:** intrinsic value, conversion price, premium in dollars and per cent, gearing, minimum value.
- **Trap:** the **approximate YTM** formula uses the **average** of current price and par in the denominator, not the current price alone.
- **Trap:** **EV/CF** subtracts cash; **EV/EBITDA** subtracts cash **and** associates but adds minorities and unfunded pension.
- **Trap:** the **Sortino** denominator is **downside** volatility, not total standard deviation.
