# CM-EIP — Study Guide

## Capital Markets – Excluded Investment Products – Securities, Collective Investment Schemes (EIP) and Foreign Exchange

A guide to the CM-EIP examination: the securities, collective investment scheme and foreign exchange products an adviser may recommend to retail clients, and the analysis used to value them. It condenses the official study guide into what the examination tests, section by section.

- **Exam:** 90 multiple-choice and multiple-response questions in 2 hours 30 minutes, including 3 case studies carrying 9 questions (3 per case study); **70% to pass**.
- **Currency of the material:** the official guide is version 1.1 (March 2025). Check the current MAS notices, SGX and SGX-DT rules, and each product's prospectus or Product Highlights Sheet before relying on a rule or specification.
- This is a study aid, not a replacement for the prospectus, Product Highlights Sheet, trust deed, insurance policy or current law. If you will conduct regulated activity, you must also satisfy the MAS representative-notification and competency requirements under Notice SFA 04-N22.

## How to use this guide

1. Learn what the investor owns before learning how it is priced. Every product in this syllabus is one of four claims: a deposit, a debt claim, an equity claim, or a contract whose value derives from something else.
2. For every product, ask: what is the underlying, what does the investor own, what drives the return, what can be lost, and who is the counterparty?
3. Work the calculations in Chapter 2 and Chapter 7 by hand until the arithmetic is automatic. The examination supplies a formulae sheet but not the judgement about which formula applies.
4. Practise the case studies in Chapter 12 last. They test the same method as the chapters, applied to a named investor with a stated objective.
5. Use `CMEIP_QUESTIONS.md` only after reading the relevant section; its answers explain the reasoning rather than merely naming a letter.

## Examination orientation

- The examination tests knowledge, understanding and application of Excluded Investment Products.
- Format: 90 multiple-choice and multiple-response questions in 2 hours 30 minutes, including 3 case studies of 9 questions; the passing mark is 70%.
- Unless a question says otherwise, use a **365-day** convention for Singapore Government Securities and a **360-day** convention for all other products.
- A non-programmable financial calculator may be used and a formulae sheet is provided.
- Scope: financial markets and instruments, risk and return measurement, the drivers of market movements, foreign exchange, company and financial-statement analysis, equities, deposits and fixed income, portfolio management, ETFs, unit trusts, REITs, insurance, warrants, and technical and quantitative analysis.

## Master mental model

Every product in this syllabus is a claim on someone else's cash flow, or a contract that transfers an exposure. Classify the product before analysing it.

| Claim type | Products in this syllabus | What the investor owns | Primary driver of return | What can be lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Deposit** | Savings and fixed deposits, currency deposits, structured deposits | A debt claim on a bank | Contracted interest rate | Principal above the insured limit if the bank fails |
| **Money market** | Treasury bills, commercial paper, CDs, banker's acceptances, repos | A short-dated debt claim | Discount to face value | Issuer default; rollover risk |
| **Bond** | Government, corporate, convertible, covered, high-yield, zero-coupon | A contractual debt claim | Coupon plus price change | Principal on default; price on a rate rise |
| **Equity** | Ordinary and preference shares, depositary receipts | Residual ownership | Earnings growth and dividends | Entire investment, ranking last |
| **Fund** | Unit trusts, ETFs, REITs, business trusts, hedge and private equity funds | Units in a pooled vehicle | The underlying portfolio, less fees | Unit value; liquidity may be restricted |
| **Insurance** | Whole life, endowment, annuities | A contract with an insurer | Insurer's crediting rate and bonuses | Surrender value below premiums paid |
| **Derivative-linked** | Warrants, structured warrants | A contract, not ownership | Movement in the underlying, less time decay | Premium paid; gearing magnifies the loss |

## Conventions used throughout

- **Money** is written as plain text (`S$10,000`, `USD1,027,500`), never as maths, so it renders literally.
- **Formulas** are written as LaTeX and rendered by KaTeX. Every symbol is defined immediately after the formula.
- **Percentages** are quoted to the precision the source uses; do not round intermediate steps in a calculation question.
- **Dates and thresholds** are reproduced as printed. Where a rule has changed, the current figure is given and the change is noted once.
