# Appendix D — Glossary

Terms are grouped by the chapter that uses them, so a term can be found from the topic you are revising rather than from its first letter. Each group ends with the chapter reference.

## Investor classification and market structure

- **Accredited investor (AI)** — an individual or entity meeting the SFA s 4A wealth or income tests, presumed financially savvy and able to bear more risk, with access to more complex products. **Chapter 1.**
- **Expert investor (EI)** — a person whose business involves acquiring, disposing of or holding capital markets products; protected less than a retail investor because of occupational knowledge, not wealth. **Chapter 1.**
- **Institutional investor (II)** — a government, statutory board, bank, insurer, CMS licensee, approved exchange, pension fund or CIS; the widest product access. **Chapter 1.**
- **Retail investor** — anyone outside the AI, EI and II definitions; the highest level of protective regulation. **Chapter 1.**
- **Primary market** — where new securities are issued and the **issuer** receives the cash. **Chapter 1.**
- **Secondary market** — where already-issued securities trade and the **selling investor** receives the cash; supplies liquidity. **Chapter 1.**
- **Marketability** — the likelihood of selling a security quickly; necessary but not sufficient for liquidity. **Chapter 1.**
- **Price continuity** — prices do not change much between transactions absent new information. **Chapter 1.**
- **Internal efficiency** — low transaction costs across reaching the market, brokerage and transfer. **Chapter 1.**
- **Price discovery** — the process by which trading establishes a transparent price; the most important function of a market. **Chapter 1.**
- **Dealer** — trades as principal, has a vested interest in the transaction and earns the bid-offer spread. **Chapter 1.**
- **Broker** — acts as agent for a commission and does not take the other side. **Chapter 1.**

## Risk, return and time value

- **Total return** — current income plus capital gain or loss, divided by the purchase price. **Chapter 2.**
- **Expected rate of return** — the probability-weighted average of possible returns. **Chapter 2.**
- **Real rate of return** — the nominal return adjusted for inflation: $$(1 + r)/(1 + \text{inflation}) - 1$$. **Chapter 2.**
- **Currency-adjusted return** — the return restated in the investor's reference currency; divide when the reference currency is first in the pair, multiply when it is second. **Chapter 2.**
- **Internal rate of return (IRR)** — the rate equating all cash outflows and inflows at one point in time; the **money-weighted** return. **Chapter 2.**
- **Compounded annual growth rate (CAGR)** — the effective annual rate on the initial cash flow; the **time-weighted** return. **Chapter 2.**
- **Variance and standard deviation** — measures of the dispersion of returns from the expected return. **Chapter 2.**
- **Volatility** — the standard deviation of the security's returns; historical volatility differs from implied volatility. **Chapter 2.**
- **Coefficient of variation (CV)** — risk per unit of expected return. **Chapter 2.**
- **Z-score** — the number of standard deviations a return deviates from the expected return. **Chapter 2.**
- **Value at risk (VaR)** — the loss at a stated tail probability, for example 2.5% or 0.5%. **Chapter 2.**
- **Systematic risk** — market-wide risk that cannot be diversified away but can be hedged. **Chapters 2, 8.**
- **Non-systematic (firm-specific) risk** — risk peculiar to a firm or industry, reducible to near zero by diversification. **Chapters 2, 8.**
- **Basis point** — 0.01%, or 0.0001. **Chapters 2, 7.**

## Market drivers

- **Fiscal policy** — government expenditure and taxation used to influence aggregate demand and supply. **Chapter 3.**
- **Monetary policy** — management of money supply and the cost of funding. **Chapter 3.**
- **Multiplier effect** — fiscal spending rippling through industries, raising incomes and further consumption. **Chapter 3.**
- **Leading indicator** — peaks and troughs **precede** the economy; a short-term predictor. **Chapter 3.**
- **Coincident indicator** — peaks and troughs **align with** the economy; reflects the present. **Chapter 3.**
- **Lagging indicator** — peaks and troughs **follow** the economy; confirms turning points. **Chapter 3.**
- **Purchasing Managers' Index (PMI)** — a leading indicator; **50** divides expansion from contraction. **Chapter 3.**
- **Industry life cycle** — early development, rapid expansion, mature growth, stabilisation, decline. **Chapter 3.**
- **Porter's five forces** — rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, buyer power, supplier power. **Chapter 3.**
- **Herd instinct** — following the crowd without independent decision-making; strongest when information is scarce. **Chapter 3.**
- **Loss aversion** — from prospect theory; preferring less uncertainty for gains and more for losses, so investors sell winners and hold losers. **Chapter 3.**
- **Cognitive bias** — concluding from past experience, preferences or predetermined beliefs; confirmation bias is the common form. **Chapter 3.**

## Foreign exchange

- **Base currency** — the first currency of a pair; the investor's reference currency. **Chapter 4.**
- **Counter (term) currency** — the second currency of a pair. **Chapter 4.**
- **Pip** — 0.0001, the fourth decimal place; the smallest FX price movement. **Chapter 4.**
- **Bid** — the price at which the dealer **buys**. **Chapter 4.**
- **Offer (ask)** — the price at which the dealer **sells**. **Chapter 4.**
- **Direct quote** — foreign currency per unit of domestic currency. **Chapter 4.**
- **Indirect quote** — domestic currency per unit of foreign currency; the reciprocal of the direct quote. **Chapter 4.**
- **Cross rate** — a rate between two non-USD currencies derived from two USD rates. **Chapter 4.**
- **Forward points (swap points)** — the interest differential expressed in FX rate terms; added for a premium, subtracted for a discount. **Chapter 4.**
- **Interest rate parity** — the forward premium or discount equals the interest rate differential for the same maturity. **Chapter 4.**
- **Covered interest arbitrage** — borrowing in one currency, investing in another and covering forward to lock a risk-free profit when parity is violated. **Chapter 4.**
- **Non-deliverable forward (NDF)** — a cash-settled forward with no delivery of the domestic currency and no exchange of notional. **Chapter 4.**
- **Carry trade** — borrowing in a low-yield currency to invest in a higher-yield one, capturing the differential. **Chapters 4, 12.**
- **Continuous Linked Settlement (CLS)** — a settlement mechanism that reduces FX delivery risk. **Chapter 4.**

## Company analysis

- **Balance sheet** — a statement of financial position at a point in time: assets, liabilities and shareholders' equity. **Chapter 5.**
- **Common size statement** — every item expressed as a percentage of total assets or of sales. **Chapter 5.**
- **Goodwill** — the premium paid above the book value of an acquired company; written down against equity reserves. **Chapter 5.**
- **Treasury stock** — shares bought back and retired, benefiting remaining shareholders. **Chapter 5.**
- **Hidden reserves** — value not reflected in book value, from rezoned land or provisions not written back. **Chapters 5, 6.**
- **Current ratio** — current assets / current liabilities. **Chapter 5.**
- **Quick (acid test) ratio** — current assets excluding inventory, over current liabilities. **Chapter 5.**
- **Gearing (debt-to-equity)** — total debt / total shareholders' equity. **Chapter 5.**
- **Interest coverage** — EBIT / interest expense. **Chapter 5.**
- **Total fixed charge coverage** — earnings before interest, taxes and lease payments over interest plus lease payments plus the grossed-up preferred dividend. **Chapter 5.**
- **Cash conversion cycle** — the period between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. **Chapter 5.**
- **Window dressing** — manipulating reporting-date ratios by collecting receivables early, cutting inventory and delaying purchases. **Chapter 5.**
- **Quality of earnings** — realistic, repeatable earnings supported by conservative accounting and adequate provisioning. **Chapter 5.**

## Equity securities

- **Residual claim** — ordinary shareholders are paid last on liquidation. **Chapter 6.**
- **Limited liability** — shareholders can lose only their original investment. **Chapter 6.**
- **Preference share** — a hybrid with a fixed dividend, no vote except in distress, ranked above ordinary and below bonds. **Chapter 6.**
- **Rights issue** — new capital raised from existing shareholders pro rata; prevents dilution. **Chapter 6.**
- **Bonus issue** — additional shares from reserves; raises no capital. **Chapter 6.**
- **Stock split** — divides existing shares to lower the unit price; raises no capital. **Chapter 6.**
- **Reverse stock split** — consolidates shares, often to escape penny-stock restrictions. **Chapter 6.**
- **Value-weighted index** — weights by market capitalisation; adjusts automatically for capital changes. **Chapter 6.**
- **Price-weighted index** — arithmetic average of prices; high-priced shares dominate. **Chapter 6.**
- **Equal-weighted index** — all constituents carry equal weight. **Chapter 6.**
- **Efficient frontier** — the set of portfolios offering the highest return for each level of risk. **Chapters 6, 8.**
- **Book value** — assets net of liabilities as stated on the balance sheet. **Chapter 6.**
- **Liquidation value** — the value realised on a forced sale of assets. **Chapter 6.**
- **Revised net asset value (RNAV)** — book value restated to market value. **Chapter 6.**
- **Free cash flow to firm (FCFF)** — after-tax operating cash flow less capital expenditure, plus non-cash charges. **Chapter 6.**
- **WACC** — the weighted average of the cost of equity and after-tax cost of debt. **Chapter 6.**
- **Terminal value** — the present value at the end of the projection period of all subsequent cash flows. **Chapter 6.**
- **P/E ratio** — price per share / earnings per share; trailing or leading. **Chapter 6.**
- **PEG ratio** — P/E divided by expected EPS growth; below 1 suggests undervaluation. **Chapters 6, 12.**
- **P/BV ratio** — price per share / book value per share; suited to banks, insurers and property companies. **Chapter 6.**
- **Enterprise value (EV)** — market capitalisation plus debt and preferred shares, less cash. **Chapter 6.**
- **EV/EBITDA** — the enterprise multiple; unaffected by capital structure. **Chapter 6.**
- **Earnings yield** — the inverse of P/E; compared with bond yields to give the **yield gap**. **Chapter 6.**
- **Depositary receipt (DR)** — a security trading in an investor's home market representing a foreign underlying share. **Chapter 6.**
- **ADR levels** — Level 1 OTC, Level 2 exchange-listed, Level 3 exchange-listed with capital raising. **Chapter 6.**
- **Greenshoe (over-allotment) option** — lets underwriters stabilise an IPO price by buying back shares. **Chapter 6.**
- **Claw-back** — reallocation of IPO shares between institutional and retail tranches. **Chapter 6.**

## Fixed income

- **Par (face) value** — the redemption value repaid at maturity; usually $1,000. **Chapter 7.**
- **Coupon rate** — the periodic interest rate applied to par value. **Chapter 7.**
- **Term bond** — a single maturity date. **Chapter 7.**
- **Serial bond** — several maturity dates. **Chapter 7.**
- **Bank discount yield** — $$(D/F)(360/t)$$; based on face value, so not a true return. **Chapter 7.**
- **Accrued interest** — interest earned since the last coupon, paid by the buyer to the seller. **Chapter 7.**
- **Eurobond** — underwritten internationally, offered simultaneously in several countries, issued outside any single jurisdiction. **Chapter 7.**
- **Zero-coupon bond** — no coupons, sold at a deep discount; no reinvestment risk, highest convexity. **Chapter 7.**
- **Floating rate bond** — coupon reset periodically against a benchmark such as SIBOR. **Chapter 7.**
- **Covered bond** — highly rated paper backed by bank assets; no default since 1769. **Chapter 7.**
- **High-yield (junk) bond** — rated below BBB; trades on creditworthiness rather than rates. **Chapter 7.**
- **Payment-in-kind (PIK) bond** — the issuer may pay cash or issue a similar bond. **Chapter 7.**
- **Perpetual bond** — no maturity; ranks before equities on default. **Chapter 7.**
- **Call provision** — the issuer's right to redeem early; raises the yield. **Chapter 7.**
- **Put provision** — the holder's right to sell back at par; lowers the yield. **Chapter 7.**
- **Sinking fund** — annual retirement of part of the issue; reduces credit risk. **Chapter 7.**
- **Credit spread** — the yield difference between bonds of the same maturity and different credit quality. **Chapter 7.**
- **Credit default swap (CDS)** — a contract transferring default risk for a premium. **Chapter 7.**
- **Collateralised debt obligation (CDO)** — pooled bonds (CBO) or loans (CLO) repackaged and sold in tranches. **Chapter 7.**
- **Duration** — the linear price sensitivity to small yield changes; higher for lower coupons, lower yields and longer maturities. **Chapter 7.**
- **Convexity** — the curvature of the price-yield relationship; higher for lower coupons, highest for zero-coupon bonds. **Chapter 7.**
- **PVBP (PV01)** — the change in portfolio value for a one basis point rate move. **Chapter 7.**
- **Current yield** — annual interest / price; ignores capital gain or loss. **Chapter 7.**
- **Yield-to-maturity (YTM)** — the discount rate equating present value to price. **Chapter 7.**
- **Bond-equivalent yield** — the semi-annual YTM doubled. **Chapter 7.**
- **Taxable equivalent yield (TEY)** — tax-exempt yield / (1 − marginal tax rate). **Chapter 7.**
- **Reinvestment risk** — the risk that coupons cannot be reinvested at the same rate; rises with coupon and maturity. **Chapter 7.**
- **Term structure (yield curve)** — the relationship between YTM and maturity. **Chapter 7.**
- **Expectations theory** — the curve's shape reflects interest rate expectations. **Chapter 7.**
- **Liquidity preference theory** — the curve is always upward sloping because long maturities need a premium. **Chapter 7.**
- **Market segmentation theory** — each maturity's yield is set by supply and demand in that segment. **Chapter 7.**
- **Conversion parity price** — market price of the convertible / conversion ratio. **Chapter 7.**
- **Conversion premium** — the excess of the convertible's market price over its conversion value. **Chapter 7.**
- **Breakeven time** — conversion premium / (bond income − dividend income). **Chapter 7.**

## Portfolio management

- **Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH)** — prices fully reflect available information; weak, semi-strong and strong forms. **Chapter 8.**
- **Random walk** — price movements are independent over time. **Chapters 8, 11.**
- **Passive strategy** — buy-and-hold or index tracking, minimising cost. **Chapter 8.**
- **Active strategy** — security selection, sector rotation and market timing to beat a benchmark. **Chapter 8.**
- **Correlation coefficient (ρ)** — the extent to which two securities' returns move together; +1 to −1. **Chapter 8.**
- **Market portfolio (M)** — the fully diversified portfolio of all risky assets, containing only systematic risk. **Chapter 8.**
- **Capital market line (CML)** — the risk-return trade-off for **efficient** portfolios, using total risk. **Chapter 8.**
- **Security market line (SML)** — the risk-return trade-off for **all** assets, using beta. **Chapter 8.**
- **Beta** — the sensitivity of a security's return to the market; the market's beta is 1. **Chapter 8.**
- **Alpha** — the excess of actual return over the model-predicted return. **Chapter 8.**
- **Arbitrage pricing theory (APT)** — a multifactor equilibrium model; factors must be pervasive, influence expected return and be unpredictable. **Chapter 8.**
- **Investment policy statement (IPS)** — the client's mandate, recording objectives and constraints. **Chapter 8.**
- **Strategic asset allocation (SAA)** — the long-run policy mix, a constant-mix strategy. **Chapter 8.**
- **Tactical asset allocation (TAA)** — short-run overweighting and underweighting within SAA ranges. **Chapter 8.**
- **Dollar-weighted return (DWR)** — the IRR; sensitive to the size and timing of cash flows. **Chapter 8.**
- **Time-weighted return (TWR)** — links sub-period returns; unaffected by client cash flows, so preferred for judging managers. **Chapter 8.**
- **Modified Dietz method** — approximates TWR by weighting each cash flow by the time held. **Chapter 8.**
- **GIPS** — the Global Investment Performance Standards; require total return, TWR, monthly valuation and disclosure. **Chapter 8.**
- **Sharpe ratio** — excess return per unit of **total** risk. **Chapter 8.**
- **Treynor ratio** — excess return per unit of **systematic** risk. **Chapter 8.**
- **Jensen's alpha** — actual return less the CAPM-expected return. **Chapters 8, 12.**
- **Information ratio** — alpha per unit of excess risk. **Chapter 8.**
- **Sortino ratio** — excess return per unit of **downside** volatility. **Chapter 8.**
- **Bogey** — the passive benchmark against which attribution is measured. **Chapter 8.**
- **Performance attribution** — decomposing excess return into asset allocation and selection components. **Chapter 8.**

## Funds, REITs and insurance

- **Collective investment scheme (CIS)** — a pooled investment vehicle governed by the Code on CIS. **Chapter 9.**
- **Variable Capital Company (VCC)** — a fund structure allowing free issue and redemption of shares, dividends from capital, and umbrella sub-funds. **Chapter 9.**
- **Exchange-traded fund (ETF)** — a passively managed fund tracking an index, traded during exchange hours. **Chapter 9.**
- **Net asset value (NAV)** — fund assets less liabilities, divided by units outstanding. **Chapter 9.**
- **Physical (cash) replication** — holding the actual index securities, by full replication or representative sampling. **Chapter 9.**
- **Synthetic replication** — using swaps and derivatives to mimic the index; lower tracking error, higher counterparty risk. **Chapter 9.**
- **Tracking error** — the divergence between an ETF's return and its index. **Chapter 9.**
- **Smart beta** — weighting securities on performance criteria rather than market capitalisation. **Chapter 9.**
- **Securities lending** — lending portfolio shares for collateral and interest; creates counterparty risk. **Chapter 9.**
- **Cash equitisation** — converting cash to equities via ETFs to reduce cash drag. **Chapter 9.**
- **Unit trust** — a CIS pooling funds under an independent trustee. **Chapter 9.**
- **Open-end fund** — issues units continuously at NAV. **Chapter 9.**
- **Closed-end fund** — a fixed number of units priced by market demand and supply. **Chapter 9.**
- **Offer price** — the price at which an investor buys units; NAV plus purchase expenses, cash, accrued income and the initial charge. **Chapter 9.**
- **Bid price** — the price at which an investor sells units; NAV less sale expenses, plus cash and accrued income. **Chapter 9.**
- **Front-end load** — a sales charge of 1.5–5% of NAV. **Chapter 9.**
- **Back-end load** — a redemption fee, often waived after a holding period. **Chapter 9.**
- **High watermark** — the profit level a manager must exceed before a performance fee is paid. **Chapter 9.**
- **Total expense ratio** — total expenses including management fees, over NAV. **Chapter 9.**
- **Retrocession** — a distribution fee paid by the fund house to the distributor; a conflict of interest that reduces NAV. **Chapter 9.**
- **REIT** — a CIS investing at least 75% in income-producing real estate and distributing at least 90% of taxable income. **Chapter 9.**
- **Business trust** — a hybrid structure under the Business Trusts Act 2004, not a separate legal entity, with a single trustee-manager. **Chapter 9.**
- **Stapled security** — two or more securities traded as one instrument. **Chapter 9.**
- **UCITS** — the EU directive providing a passport for cross-border fund distribution. **Chapter 9.**
- **Hedge fund** — a fund using short selling, derivatives and leverage with absolute return targets. **Chapter 9.**
- **Lock-up period** — a period during which hedge fund investors cannot redeem. **Chapter 9.**
- **Gate provision** — a limit on withdrawals at a given redemption date. **Chapter 9.**
- **Private equity J-curve** — negative early cash flows during the 5–6 year investment period, with harvest from year 6. **Chapter 9.**
- **Whole life policy** — life cover plus cash value, payable on death whenever it occurs. **Chapter 9.**
- **Endowment policy** — payable on maturity or death, whichever is earlier. **Chapter 9.**
- **With-profits policy** — benefits linked **indirectly** through annual valuation and a reversionary bonus. **Chapter 9.**
- **Investment-linked policy** — policy values linked **directly** to underlying unit values. **Chapter 9.**
- **Immediate annuity** — payments begin at purchase, funded by a single premium. **Chapter 9.**
- **Deferred annuity** — payments begin at a future date. **Chapter 9.**
- **Dollar cost averaging** — investing equal amounts over time, accumulating more units when prices are low. **Chapter 9.**

## Warrants

- **Warrant** — a right, not an obligation, to buy or sell a specified number of securities at a fixed price within a specified period. **Chapter 10.**
- **Company warrant** — issued by the company on its own shares; call only; new shares issued on exercise. **Chapter 10.**
- **Structured warrant** — issued by a third party such as a bank; call or put; cash-settled; no new shares. **Chapter 10.**
- **Conversion ratio** — the number of warrants needed to convert into one share. **Chapter 10.**
- **Intrinsic value** — (SP − EP)/n when in-the-money; zero otherwise. **Chapter 10.**
- **Conversion price** — the total cost of one share via the warrant: n × WP + EP. **Chapter 10.**
- **Warrant premium** — the extra cost of the warrant route over buying the share outright. **Chapter 10.**
- **Gearing ratio** — SP / (n × WP); the leverage the warrant provides. **Chapter 10.**

## Technical and quantitative analysis

- **Technical analysis** — forecasting price movements from historical market data; not concerned with fair value. **Chapter 11.**
- **Advance-decline (A/D) line** — cumulative advances minus declines across all securities; measures market breadth. **Chapter 11.**
- **Moving average** — the average closing price over a period; smooths erratic price changes. **Chapter 11.**
- **MACD** — the difference between a fast and a slow EMA, with a signal line; commonly (12,26,9). **Chapter 11.**
- **Bollinger Bands** — a 20-day SMA plus and minus two standard deviations. **Chapter 11.**
- **Support level** — a price range where a substantial increase in demand is expected. **Chapter 11.**
- **Resistance level** — a price range where buyers refuse to pay more and sellers take profits; the overhang. **Chapter 11.**
- **Breakout** — a move through support or resistance, confirming the trend. **Chapter 11.**
- **Dow Theory** — primary, secondary and daily movements; confirmed when the DJIA and DJTA agree. **Chapter 11.**
- **Relative Strength Index (RSI)** — a momentum indicator from 0 to 100; above 70 overbought, below 30 oversold. **Chapter 11.**
- **Head and shoulders** — a reversal pattern at a peak; the inverse form marks a bottom. **Chapter 11.**
- **Double top / bottom** — a reversal pattern confirmed when the neckline breaks. **Chapter 11.**
- **Consolidation rectangle** — a sideways continuation pattern. **Chapter 11.**
- **Triangle** — symmetrical (either direction), ascending (bullish) or descending (bearish). **Chapter 11.**
- **Flag and pennant** — short continuation patterns after a sharp move; parallel lines versus converging lines. **Chapter 11.**
- **Wedge** — converging lines both moving the same direction; a rising wedge is bearish. **Chapter 11.**
- **Gap** — a price range with no transactions; breakaway, runaway or exhaustion. **Chapter 11.**
- **Elliott Wave Theory** — 5-wave advances and 3-wave retreats exhibiting the Golden Ratio 1.618. **Chapter 11.**
- **Quantitative analysis** — abstracting market reality into models to find price anomalies and arbitrage. **Chapter 11.**

- **NDF: cash-settled, no delivery, no notional exchange.**

- **Goodwill write-downs shrink equity and raise ROE.**

- **Duration rises with lower coupon, lower yield and longer maturity.**

- **RSI above 70 overbought, below 30 oversold.**

- **Trap:** a **beta of zero** does not make an asset risk-free.
- **Trap:** an **unrated** bond is not automatically a default risk.
