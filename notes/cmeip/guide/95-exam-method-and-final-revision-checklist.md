# Exam method and final revision checklist

The CM-EIP paper is **90 questions in 2 hours 30 minutes** — about **100 seconds per question**, including the three case studies that carry nine questions between them. The pass mark is **70%**, so **63 correct answers**. That leaves room for 27 errors and no more, which means the method matters as much as the knowledge.

## How to work the paper

1. **Answer every question.** There is no penalty for a wrong answer, so never leave a blank. If time is short, fill the remaining answers with your best judgement and return only if time allows.
2. **Read the stem for the qualifier first.** Words such as *least likely*, *most appropriate*, *not*, *except* and *which of the following is TRUE* decide the answer more often than the technical content does.
3. **Classify the question before computing.** Most calculation questions name the measure they want. Identify whether it is a return measure, a risk measure, a valuation ratio, a bond measure or a warrant measure, then recall the formula from Appendix B.
4. **Check the convention.** **365-day** for Singapore Government Securities, **360-day** for everything else. Semi-annual coupons double the periods and halve the rate. Currency adjustment divides when the reference currency is first in the pair and multiplies when it is second.
5. **Sanity-check the magnitude.** A bond priced at $1,147 against a $1,000 par with an 8% coupon and a 6% yield is plausible; $1,014 is not. A P/E of 12x on a mature industrial and 37x on a high-growth semiconductor are both plausible; 370x is not.
6. **For multiple-response questions, evaluate each option independently.** Do not look for a pattern in the letters. A statement is true or false on its own merits.
7. **Do the case studies last if they are grouped at the end**, or first if you prefer to bank the applied marks while fresh. Either way, read the scenario once and note the client's objective, horizon, risk tolerance and any constraint — the questions usually turn on one of those.
8. **Leave the hardest calculation for a second pass.** Mark it, move on, and return. Two minutes lost on one question costs two easier questions.

## The ten distinctions that decide the most marks

| Distinction | The rule |
|---|---|
| **Primary vs secondary market** | Primary: the **issuer** receives the cash. Secondary: the **selling investor** does |
| **Dealer vs broker** | Dealer trades as principal and earns the **spread**; broker acts as agent for a **commission** |
| **Money market vs capital market** | Money market: **≤ 1 year**. Capital market: **> 1 year** |
| **Direct vs indirect quote** | Direct: foreign/domestic. Indirect: domestic/foreign — the **reciprocal** |
| **Discount vs premium bond** | Coupon **below** yield → discount. Coupon **above** yield → premium. Equal → par |
| **Duration vs convexity** | Duration is the **linear** first-order sensitivity; convexity is the **curvature**, the second derivative |
| **CML vs SML** | CML: **efficient portfolios**, **total risk**. SML: **all assets**, **beta** |
| **DWR vs TWR** | DWR = IRR, driven by client cash flows. TWR = manager skill, unaffected by them |
| **REIT vs business trust** | REIT: **90%** distribution, **45%** gearing cap, real estate only. Business trust: **no minimum**, **no cap**, any business |
| **Company vs structured warrant** | Company: **call only**, **new shares issued**, years. Structured: **call or put**, **cash-settled**, 6–12 months |

## Before the examination, be able to explain the difference between

- An **institutional**, **accredited**, **expert** and **retail** investor, and the statutory thresholds for each.
- A **financial asset** and a **real asset**, and why the distinction matters to an adviser.
- **Money market** and **capital market** instruments, and which are quoted on a discount basis.
- **Total return**, **expected return**, **annualised return**, **real return**, **currency-adjusted return** and **IRR** — and which question each answers.
- **Variance**, **standard deviation**, **coefficient of variation** and **value at risk**.
- **Systematic** and **non-systematic** risk, and how each is managed.
- **Fiscal** and **monetary** policy, and how each transmits to asset prices.
- **Leading**, **coincident** and **lagging** indicators, with one example of each.
- **Direct**, **indirect** and **cross** FX quotations, and the bid-offer spread.
- **Forward points** and **interest rate parity**, and why the higher-rate currency trades at a forward discount.
- A **non-deliverable forward** and why it exists.
- **Qualitative** and **quantitative** company analysis.
- The **balance sheet**, **income statement** and **cash flow statement**, and what each reveals that the others do not.
- **Liquidity**, **leverage**, **efficiency** and **profitability** ratios, and which use average balances.
- **Ordinary** and **preference** shares, and the claim order on liquidation.
- A **rights issue**, **bonus issue**, **stock split** and **reverse split**.
- **Value-weighted**, **price-weighted** and **equal-weighted** indices.
- **Net asset**, **absolute (DCF)** and **relative** valuation.
- **P/E**, **PEG**, **P/BV**, **P/Sales**, **EV/CF** and **EV/EBITDA**, and when each is appropriate.
- **Deposits**, **money market instruments** and **bonds**, and the deposit insurance limits.
- **Duration** and **convexity**, and what each implies for price risk.
- **Current yield**, **YTM**, **bond-equivalent yield** and **taxable equivalent yield**.
- The **expectations**, **liquidity preference** and **market segmentation** theories of the yield curve.
- **Conversion value**, **conversion parity price**, **conversion premium** and **breakeven time** for a convertible bond.
- The **weak**, **semi-strong** and **strong** forms of the efficient market hypothesis.
- **Passive** and **active** management, and the three active strategies.
- **Strategic** and **tactical** asset allocation.
- The **Sharpe**, **Treynor**, **Jensen**, **information** and **Sortino** measures, and which risk each uses.
- **Performance attribution** and the difference between allocation and selection effects.
- An **ETF**, a **unit trust**, a **REIT**, a **business trust** and a **stapled security**.
- **Whole life**, **endowment**, **with-profits** and **investment-linked** insurance.
- An **immediate** and a **deferred** annuity, and why annuities hedge longevity risk.
- **Dollar cost averaging** and **market timing**.
- A **company warrant** and a **structured warrant**, and the four warrant valuation measures.
- **Technical analysis** and **fundamental analysis**, and the assumptions that separate them.
- **Support**, **resistance**, **breakout** and the main reversal and continuation patterns.

## Final week

- **Re-read every chapter's Exam cues block.** They are written to be the last thing you read.
- **Work the Appendix A review questions** without looking at the answers, then check.
- **Rehearse the formulae sheet** in Appendix B until you can locate any formula within seconds. It is provided in the examination, so the skill is retrieval, not memorisation.
- **Practise the case studies in Chapter 12** as full sets of three questions, timed.
- **Take at least two full mock exams** on this site at the real length and pass mark.
- **Confirm the logistics:** original NRIC or passport, a **non-programmable** calculator, arrival about 30 minutes early, and no notes or smart devices.
