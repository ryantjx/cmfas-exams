# Ethics, codes and standards for securities dealing

Ethics as the standard above the legal minimum. The examination tests the client-centred framework, the ethical threats, the six standards of professional conduct, and the six-step resolution process.

## Learning Objectives

The candidate should be able to:

- Define ethics and explain why it matters to market integrity.
- Apply the four-component ethical framework to a securities-dealing situation.
- Identify the threats that create an ethical dilemma.
- Recall the six code-of-ethics principles and the six standards of professional conduct.
- Apply the six-step process to resolve a dilemma, and document it.

## 4.1 Introduction

### 4.1.1 Definition of Ethics in the Capital Markets Industry

Ethics is **a set of moral principles or rules of conduct that provide guidance for behaviour when it affects others**. A dictionary adds the alternatives of a **system of moral principles** and a **framework of moral precepts or rules of conduct** followed by an individual.

Moral frameworks differ with education, belief and culture, so ethics is **beyond regulation and law** — doing the right thing even when no one is watching, to avoid hurting others. A representative must know the professional codes applicable to securities dealing and the behaviour expected with clients and other participants.

### 4.1.2 The Importance of Ethics

Unethical behaviour damages the individual's career, **investors' confidence**, **public trust** and, in severe cases, causes **market contagion**. False markets, cornering a stock, Ponzi schemes, mis-selling of sub-prime bonds, accounting fraud, market manipulation and insider-trading schemes have all destabilised markets — and with cross-border flows and international product distribution, that destabilisation is **not contained within domestic markets**.

Well-functioning markets allocate capital to the most attractive prospects, matching issuers seeking capital with investors seeking returns. That matching needs **fair and transparent mechanisms**, which rest on an ethical foundation. Regulators responded to successive misconduct scandals with reform, but reform alone does not substitute for the individual's judgement.

## 4.2 The Ethical Framework

Codes of ethics generally guide best practice rather than impose law. Where a code is breached **and** the law is not enforced, misconduct can still attract **penalties on the representative and the firm**, plus **civil and criminal liability**. Organisations can adopt the same framework to build a supportive culture.

The framework has **four components**, all aimed at serving the client's best interests with integrity:

| Component | What it requires |
|---|---|
| **1. Understand products and services** | Thorough knowledge of the features and **risks** offered, and whether the product suits the client's investment profile and risk tolerance |
| **2. Analyse risks** | Discuss the risks of the proposed product or service **in detail** with the client, and ensure advice reflects the client's risk tolerance and financial capacity |
| **3. Execute solutions** | Make the process **smooth and efficient**, and above all **transparent and fair** |
| **4. Serve customers** | Honesty and integrity, putting the client's interest first, with the firm's reputation and market integrity protected alongside |

Unethical behaviour is commonly driven by **client pressure**, **weak knowledge or supervision**, **personal financial or status incentives**, or **peer and senior pressure**.

## 4.3 Ethical Dilemmas

An ethical dilemma is a conflict between a proposed action and ethical standards, rules or professional obligations. The **key threats** are:

- **Self-interest** — the representative's own pay, target or position.
- **Self-review** — judging one's own work or recommendation.
- **Influence** — pressure from a client, senior, peer or issuer.

Resolve facts before assumptions, and never let a **sales target** convert an unsuitable product into a recommendation.

## 4.4 Professional Ethics, Codes and Standards of Professional Conduct

Professional ethics is the set of behaviour and conduct expected of representatives in a particular industry. The code of ethics has **six principles**:

1. Act with **integrity, competence, diligence and respect**, ethically, towards the public, clients, prospective clients, employers, employees, colleagues and other market participants.
2. Place the **integrity of the profession and the client's interest above personal interest**.
3. Use **reasonable care and independent professional judgement** in analysis, recommendations, actions and other professional activity.
4. **Practise and encourage** professional and ethical conduct that reflects credit on the individual and the profession.
5. **Promote the integrity and viability** of global capital markets for the ultimate benefit of society.
6. **Maintain and improve professional competence**, and that of other investment professionals.

### 4.4.1 Standards of Professional Conduct

#### 4.4.1.1 Professionalism

| Standard | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Knowledge of the law** | Understand and comply with all applicable laws, rules and regulations — SFA, the MAS obligations under the FAA, and exchange and professional-body rules. Do not knowingly participate in or assist a violation, and **dissociate** from it |
| **Independence and objectivity** | Apply reasonable care and judgement. Do not offer, solicit or accept any **gift, benefit, compensation or consideration** that could reasonably be expected to compromise independence or objectivity. Comply with internal gifts and entertainment guidelines and resist **internal pressure**, especially during order execution |
| **Misrepresentation** | Do not knowingly misrepresent analysis, recommendations, actions or other professional activity. A misrepresentation occurs where advice is **not based on facts, is false, or omits material information** |
| **Misconduct** | Do not engage in conduct involving **dishonesty, fraud or deceit**, or any act that reflects adversely on professional reputation, integrity or competence — including lying, cheating, ignoring rules of conduct, or failing to give **client orders priority** over personal trades. Declare personal investments honestly and accurately |

#### 4.4.1.2 Integrity of the Capital Markets

Do not trade, or induce others to trade, on **material non-public information**. Typical inside information includes **earnings, mergers, asset-quality changes, new business or licences, management changes, qualified audit opinions, disputes, bankruptcy, regulatory breaches and credit-rating changes**.

#### 4.4.1.3 Duties to Clients

- **Loyalty, prudence and care** — act in the client's interest.
- **Fair dealing** — deal fairly with all clients when providing analysis, recommendations and actions.
- **Suitability** — make reasonable inquiries into objectives, financial situation, needs, risk appetite and **total portfolio** before recommending.
- **Performance presentation** — present fair, accurate and complete information, with the valuation basis and sources.
- **Confidentiality** — preserve confidentiality unless illegal activity, law or client permission permits disclosure.

Execution-related advice on **listed EIPs** requires a rationale and the prescribed disclosure; broader advice may require an **FAA appointment**.

#### 4.4.1.4 Duties to Employers

Be loyal, protect confidential information and firm opportunities, follow the code of conduct and remain **fit and proper**. Employers should provide clear policies, training, balanced remuneration and **whistleblowing channels**. Representatives must not **solicit former clients** or take client lists, trade secrets or confidential material without permission; additional compensation that conflicts with the employer needs **written consent**; supervisors must make reasonable efforts to ensure subordinates comply, and silence that could imply collusion is not acceptable.

#### 4.4.1.5 Investment Analysis, Recommendations and Actions

Use diligence, independence and thorough research, with a **reasonable basis**. Retain supporting records. Explain the investment process, material changes, limitations, risks, important factors, costs and the **distinction between fact and opinion**. Email, mobile, blogs and other media must comply with firm controls.

#### 4.4.1.6 Conflicts of Interest

Avoid conflicts, or make **full, fair, prominent and plain-language** disclosure. Examples include personal or proprietary positions, family interests, cross-department information, share ownership, outside directorships and multiple-principal activity. **Refrain from deciding while conflicted** and escalate independently. Give client trades priority over personal trades and do not negotiate private referral fees outside approved arrangements.

### 4.4.2 Compliance Procedures

Use **Chinese walls** to segregate sales, dealing, research, proprietary, risk, compliance and audit. Resist undue influence from issuers and sponsors, and control gifts and entertainment. Protect research integrity through objective analysis, personal-investment policies, **restricted lists** and compensation that does not reward compromised opinions.

## 4.5 Applying the Ethical Framework to Resolve Ethical Dilemmas

Use a **six-step** process:

1. **Gather the facts** — separate fact from assumption.
2. **Identify the ethical and regulatory issues.**
3. **Identify the affected parties and the conflicts.**
4. **Consult** the internal reporting and whistleblowing procedures.
5. **Evaluate the alternatives.**
6. **Implement the best action**, monitor it and **document the reasoning**.

Documentation proves what was known, what was considered and why the action was reasonable — which is what an investigation asks for.

## Exam cues

- **The framework is client-centred and ordered:** understand products → analyse risks objectively → execute quickly, fairly and transparently → serve with honesty and integrity.
- **Three threats:** self-interest, self-review and influence.
- **Six code principles:** integrity, competence, diligence and respect; profession and clients above self; reasonable care and independent judgement; practise and encourage; promote market integrity; maintain competence.
- **Six standards, one reflex:** knowledge of the law, independence and objectivity, no misrepresentation, no misconduct, capital-market integrity, duties to clients and employers, analysis with a reasonable basis, and conflict control — with **dissociate** as the answer to a violation you cannot stop.
- **Conflicts:** avoid, or disclose fully and prominently; do not decide while conflicted. Chinese walls segregate sales, dealing, research, proprietary, risk, compliance and audit.
- **Trap:** disclosure is not a licence — a conflict must still be controlled or avoided, and a sales target never converts an unsuitable product into a recommendation.
