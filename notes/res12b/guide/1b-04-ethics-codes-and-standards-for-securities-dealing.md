# Ethics, codes and standards for securities dealing

Ethics as the standard above the legal minimum. The examination tests the client-centred framework, the ethical threats, and the six-step resolution process.

## 4.1 Ethics and importance

Ethics are the moral principles or rules guiding behaviour when it affects others. Ethics goes beyond the law: it is doing the right thing even when no one is watching. Ethical failure damages the individual, the firm, market confidence and the wider financial ecosystem; interconnected markets let harm spread across borders.

## 4.2 Ethical framework

The framework is a client-centred cycle:

1. **Understand products/services:** know features, risks and suitability.
2. **Analyse risks objectively and independently:** consider client risk tolerance and financial capacity.
3. **Execute solutions quickly, fairly and transparently.**
4. **Serve customers with honesty and integrity.**
5. **Safeguard the firm’s reputation and market integrity.**

Unethical behaviour can be driven by client pressure, weak knowledge/supervision, personal financial or status incentives, or peer/senior pressure. Six preparation steps are: put clients first; emphasise integrity, competence, diligence and respect; exercise care and independent judgement; build trust and observe market conduct; disassociate from a problematic deal; and seek compliance/legal guidance.

## 4.3 Ethical dilemmas

An ethical dilemma is a conflict between a proposed action and ethical standards, rules or professional obligations. Key threats are self-interest, self-review and influence. Identify facts before assumptions and do not let sales targets convert an unsuitable product into a recommendation.

## 4.4 Professional ethics and standards

Core principles are integrity, competence, diligence and respect; putting profession/client interests above personal interests; reasonable care and independent judgement; conduct that reflects credit on the profession; promotion of market integrity; and continuing competence.

### 4.4.1.1 Professionalism

Know and follow applicable law, including SFA/FAA requirements and exchange rules; maintain independence/objectivity; do not misrepresent; do not engage in dishonest, fraudulent or deceptive misconduct; and maintain competence. Gifts, benefits, compensation and pressure must not compromise judgement.

### 4.4.1.2 Integrity of capital markets

Do not trade or induce others to trade on material non-public information. Typical information includes earnings, mergers, asset-quality changes, new business/licences, management changes, qualified audit opinions, disputes, bankruptcy, regulatory breaches and credit-rating changes.

### 4.4.1.3 Duties to clients

Act with loyalty, prudence and care; deal fairly; make suitable recommendations based on reasonable inquiries into objectives, financial situation, needs, risk appetite and total portfolio; present fair, accurate and complete performance information with valuation basis and sources; and preserve confidentiality unless illegal activity, law or client permission permits disclosure. Execution-related advice on listed EIPs requires rationale and prescribed disclosure; broader advice may require FAA appointment.

### 4.4.1.4 Duties to employers

Be loyal, protect confidential information and firm opportunities, follow the code of conduct and remain fit and proper. Employers should provide clear policies, training, balanced remuneration and whistleblowing channels. Representatives must not solicit former clients or take client lists, trade secrets or confidential material without permission. Additional compensation that conflicts with the employer requires written consent. Supervisors must make reasonable efforts to ensure subordinates comply; whistleblowing is expected where silence could imply collusion.

### 4.4.1.5 Investment analysis, recommendations and actions

Use diligence, independence and thorough research; have a reasonable basis; retain supporting records; explain the investment process, material changes, limitations, risks, important factors, costs and the distinction between fact and opinion. Communications through email, mobile, blogs and other media must comply with firm controls.

### 4.4.1.6 Conflicts of interest

Avoid conflicts or make full, fair, prominent and plain-language disclosure. Examples include personal/proprietary positions, family interests, cross-department information, ownership of stock, outside directorships and multiple-principal activity. Refrain from deciding when conflicted and escalate independently. Give client trades priority over personal trades and do not negotiate private referral fees outside approved arrangements.

### 4.4.2 Compliance procedures

Use Chinese walls and segregate sales, dealing, research, proprietary, risk, compliance and audit functions. Resist undue influence from issuers or sponsors and control gifts/entertainment. Protect research integrity through objective analysis, personal-investment policies, restricted lists and compensation that does not reward compromised opinions.

## 4.5 Resolving ethical dilemmas

Use a six-step process: gather facts; identify ethical/regulatory issues; identify affected parties and conflicts; consult internal reporting/whistleblowing procedures; evaluate alternatives; implement the best action, monitor and document the reasoning. Documentation proves what was known, what was considered and why the action was reasonable.

## Exam cues

- **The framework is client-centred and ordered:** understand products → analyse risks objectively → execute quickly, fairly and transparently → serve with honesty and integrity → safeguard reputation and market integrity.
- **Three threats:** self-interest, self-review and influence.
- **Six preparation steps:** clients first; integrity, competence, diligence and respect; care and independent judgement; trust and market conduct; disassociate from a problematic deal; seek compliance/legal guidance.
- **Conflicts:** avoid, or disclose fully and prominently; do not decide while conflicted. Chinese walls segregate sales, dealing, research, proprietary, risk, compliance and audit.
- **Trap:** disclosure is not a licence — a conflict must still be controlled or avoided.
