# Appendix E — Examples of Suspicious Transactions

The list of suspicious-transaction examples is reproduced once, in **[Appendix F of Part I](../chapter-1b-appendix-f-suspicious-transaction-examples/)**, from **Appendix B to the Guidelines to MAS Notice SFA04-N02**. This page states only the indicators that matter on a derivatives book.

## What differs in Part II

The Part I list is written for securities and CIS activity. On a derivatives book the same principles appear in these forms:

- **Leverage conceals origin.** A client who funds a large margin balance from an unexplained source, or who repeatedly tops up margin just before a large position, is displaying the "transactions not reconciling with usual activity" red flag in derivative form.
- **Frequent close-outs and re-openings** with no commercial rationale can move value between accounts or counterparties without a visible cash trail — the derivatives equivalent of rapid transfers between a client's own accounts.
- **Loss-making trades** that a client accepts without objection, or a pattern of deliberately losing positions, can be a value-transfer mechanism between connected parties.
- **Rollovers at off-market rates** are called out in the dealing chapter as a fraud risk; they are also an AML indicator, because they can conceal profit or loss.
- **Cash-settled products** leave no delivery trail to inspect, so **economic rationale** and **source of funds** carry the whole evidential burden.
- **Cross-border, unregulated counterparties** — an offshore SPV or an entity in a higher-risk jurisdiction — are a stronger indicator for OTC derivatives than for exchange-traded products, because the OTC leg has no exchange between the parties.

## Exam cues

- **The list is an aid, not a checklist:** one item may be innocent, a combination is indicative.
- **Test plausibility** of the client's explanation, and treat a refusal to provide normal documents as itself suspicious.
- **Trap:** a derivative with a legitimate hedging purpose can still be a laundering vehicle; the presence of a hedge rationale does not end the inquiry.
