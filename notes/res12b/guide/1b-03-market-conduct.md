# Market conduct

The prohibitions that keep markets fair, efficient, orderly and transparent. The examination tests which provision catches which behaviour, and the extraterritorial reach of the rules.

## Learning Objectives

The candidate should be able to:

- State the territorial and extraterritorial scope of the market-conduct provisions.
- Identify which provision catches a described behaviour, and the mental element it requires.
- Apply the insider-trading definitions to connected and non-connected persons.
- Distinguish false trading, manipulation, false statements and deceptive devices.
- Explain hawking, short selling, marking and specified-product lending rules.

## 3.1 Introduction

The provisions in this chapter implement the SFA's market-conduct regime. They apply to acts **in Singapore** involving products listed or quoted inside or outside Singapore, and to acts **outside Singapore** involving products listed or quoted on a Singapore organised market. This extraterritorial reach is a frequent examination point.

## 3.2 Market Misconduct under the SFA

The market-misconduct provisions share one objective — markets that are **fair, efficient, orderly and transparent** — and divide the work between them. Each targets a different mechanism:

| Provision | What it catches |
|---|---|
| False trading and market rigging | Creating a false or misleading appearance of active trading or price |
| Securities market manipulation | Transactions intended to move or stabilise a price to induce others |
| False or misleading statements | Disseminating information likely to induce dealing or move price |
| Fraudulently inducing dealing | Inducing dealing through deception |
| Manipulative and deceptive devices | The broad anti-fraud catch-all |
| Dissemination of illegal-transaction information | Circulating information that a price will move because of an illegal transaction |
| Insider trading | Dealing or communicating while in possession of inside information |

The **mental element** separates them: manipulation requires **intention to induce**, false trading may be satisfied by **recklessness**, and the anti-fraud provision needs only a scheme or device that **operates** as a deception.

## 3.3 False Trading and Market Rigging Transactions

Prohibited conduct includes creating an appearance of active trading, buying and selling without changing beneficial ownership, acting **recklessly** in a way likely to create a false or misleading market appearance, and transactions intended to mislead about price or market activity.

- **Wash sale** — a transaction with **no real change in beneficial ownership**.
- **Matched orders** — coordinated buy and sell orders entered with **knowledge of the other side**, to create a false market.

## 3.4 Securities Market Manipulation

It is prohibited to effect **two or more transactions** in securities or securities-based derivatives that raise, lower, maintain or stabilise a price with the **intention of inducing** others to trade. Trading through several customer accounts, or using fictitious orders to support a price, can be manipulative.

## 3.5 Dissemination of False or Misleading Statements and Information

It is prohibited to disseminate materially false or misleading information likely to induce subscription, buying or selling, or to raise, lower, maintain or stabilise a price, where the person **knows or should know** it is false or is **reckless** as to its truth. The medium is irrelevant — research, newsletters, electronic communications, media or word of mouth all count.

## 3.6 Fraudulently Inducing Persons to Deal in Capital Markets Products

A person must not induce or attempt to induce dealing through a knowingly misleading, false or deceptive statement; the **dishonest concealment of a material fact**; reckless publication; or storing or recording materially false information.

## 3.7 Employment of Manipulative and Deceptive Devices

Section 201 is a broad anti-fraud rule. It covers schemes or devices to defraud, practices likely to operate as fraud or deception, materially false statements, and omissions of material facts needed to make statements non-misleading.

Spoofing-like conduct in a pre-open or pre-close phase — placing large orders with no intention to execute, then cancelling them to move the price — is the type of conduct the section captures.

## 3.8 Dissemination of Information about Illegal Transactions

It is an offence to circulate information that a price will rise, fall or be maintained **because of an illegal transaction**. An illegal transaction includes false trading, rigging, manipulation, false statements, fraudulent inducement and deceptive devices. Liability may arise where the person, or an associate, **entered or purported to enter** the illegal transaction, or **received or expected a benefit** for circulating the information.

## 3.9 Insider Trading

**Inside information** is information that is **not generally available**, would be **likely to materially affect** price or value, and would **influence persons who commonly invest**.

**Generally available** information is information that is observable; publicised in a way likely to reach investors **after a reasonable dissemination period** has elapsed; or a reasonable deduction or inference from such information.

- **Connected persons** include officers, substantial shareholders and people whose professional or business relationship gives access to price-sensitive information — lawyers, accountants, bankers, investment bankers, public-relations advisers, consultants and transaction counterparties.
- **Non-connected persons** may still be liable if they know the information is non-public and price-sensitive — **regardless of the source** and **regardless of any intention not to use it**.
- Persons must not **trade**, **procure another to trade**, or **communicate** the information to facilitate trading.

**Exceptions and defences:** specified CIS redemptions; underwriter activity; legally required transactions or communications; corporate and partnership knowledge rules where **Chinese walls** apply; a person's own transaction knowledge; corporate own-transaction knowledge; parity of information; and the broker-dealer exception, where the instruction was unsolicited, no advice was given and the principal is not an associate. If in doubt, stop and consult compliance.

## 3.10 Securities Hawking

Securities hawking is offering securities, securities-based derivatives or CIS units in an **unsolicited meeting**. It prevents pressure selling and "boiler-room" practices. The prohibition does not apply in the same way to qualifying offers to **institutional or accredited investors**, nor to certain products that do not require a prospectus.

## 3.11 Other Market Conduct Rules and Guidelines

- **Short selling** — a **covered** short sale has borrowed or arranged delivery; an **uncovered (naked)** short sale has not. Short selling can improve liquidity and price formation but can increase volatility and settlement risk. **CDP** may conduct buying-in and charge the purchase cost plus the applicable penalty for failed delivery.
- **Marking sell orders** — a seller must identify a short-sale order and its quantity or value **before or when placing** it. An order that is partly owned and partly short must be **split and the relevant portion marked**. SGX publishes aggregated short-sale information.
- **Short positions** — when a net short position crosses the **MAS threshold**, the holder must report identity and business information, the position and its changes.
- **Specified-product borrowing and lending** — collateral must generally be at least **100%** of market value, **marked to market daily**, and the agreement must cover capacity (agent or principal), title transfer, collateral, corporate actions, valuation and margin shortfalls, return, termination and default, fees, governing law and jurisdiction. Risks must be explained and written understanding obtained for non-exempt customers; exemptions can apply for accredited, expert and institutional investors.

## 3.12 Penalties for Misconduct under the SFA

Market misconduct may result in criminal **fines and imprisonment**, **civil penalties**, **compensation** for other participants' losses, **licence suspension or revocation**, **disciplinary action** and **reputational damage**. Learn the categories and consequences — the **amount** of a penalty is not tested.

## Exam cues

- **Extraterritorial reach:** acts in Singapore on foreign-listed products, and acts abroad on Singapore-listed products, are both caught.
- **Intent separates the provisions:** manipulation needs **intention to induce trading**; false trading may be **reckless**; **section 201** is the broad anti-fraud catch-all.
- **Insider trading:** connected vs non-connected. A non-connected person can be liable merely knowing the information is non-public and price-sensitive, even without intent to use it.
- **Generally available** requires a reasonable dissemination period to have elapsed.
- **Hawking** targets the **unsolicited meeting**; qualifying offers to institutional and accredited investors are outside it.
- **Specified-product lending:** collateral at least **100%**, marked daily, with written understanding for non-exempt customers.
- **Trap:** the private or public nature of the information — not the source — decides non-connected liability.
