# Appendix E — Excluded Investment Products

**Excluded Investment Products (EIPs)** are the listed and simple products that fall **outside** the Specified Investment Product regime. Because EIPs are not SIPs, neither the CAR nor the CKA is required to deal in them.

The list is set out in the **Schedule to the Securities and Futures (Capital Markets Products) Regulations 2018**, and this appendix reproduces its categories.

## The categories

### Ordinary shares and share units

- **Ordinary shares** and share units, and units in a **business trust**.
- **Issuer-issued rights, options and derivatives** over such shares, share units or business-trust units — for example a rights issue, or an issuer-issued warrant over its own shares.

### Listed real-estate CIS units

Units in a **real-estate collective investment scheme** that is listed on an exchange.

### Debentures, with two carve-outs

Any **debenture** — but **not**:

- **asset-backed securities** as defined in section 262(3) of the Act; or
- **structured notes** as defined in regulation 2(1) of the Securities and Futures (Offers of Investments) (Shares and Debentures) Regulations 2005.

A plain corporate bond is an EIP. An ABS or a structured note is not.

### Immediate currency exchange

Any contract or arrangement under which one party agrees to **exchange currency at an agreed rate** with another party, where the exchange is **effected immediately**.

The word "immediately" does the work: a spot exchange is an EIP, but a forward, swap or option is not, because settlement is deferred.

### Tightly defined collective investment schemes

A scheme qualifies only if its investment policy restricts it in one of two ways:

**i.** To invest the scheme's property **only** in one or more of:

- **(A)** deposits as defined in section 4B(4) of the Banking Act 1970;
- **(B)** gold certificates, gold savings accounts or physical gold;
- **(C)** any capital markets products belonging to a class of excluded investment products;
- **(D)** any product, instrument, contract or arrangement where the investment is **solely for the purpose of hedging or efficient portfolio management**.

**ii.** Alternatively, the manager must invest only in the products in sub-paragraph (i), **except** that it may invest in other products if **both** of the following hold:

- **(BA)** there has been a **change in any written law, regulation, direction, rule or non-statutory instrument** of the jurisdiction where the scheme is constituted, operated or invests; **and**
- **(BB)** following that change, the manager is **restricted or prohibited** from investing in the products in sub-paragraph (i).

Even then, the manager must still invest the scheme's property only in the products in sub-paragraph (i).

That escape is narrow on purpose: a scheme does not become an EIP because the manager prefers a broader mandate — a change of law must force the substitution.

### Stapled products

**Two or more products in this Appendix linked together in a stapled manner**, so that one cannot be transferred or otherwise dealt without the others.

The staple must be of EIPs. Stapling an EIP to a SIP does not make the combination an EIP.

## Why the list matters operationally

**Dealing in an EIP does not require a CAR or CKA.** Suitability and disclosure obligations for advisory or discretionary services still apply, but the SIP knowledge gate does not.

**Classification must be accurate and current.** A product can move in or out of the list: a structured note is not an EIP even where it looks like a bond, and an ABS is carved out of the debenture limb.

**Outsourcing does not transfer responsibility.** Where a firm relies on a third party to classify a product, the classification remains the **CMS holder's responsibility**, and without a reliable classification system the product is treated as a SIP with the CAR applying.

## Exam cues

- **EIPs sit outside the SIP regime**, so no CAR and no CKA.
- **Ordinary shares, business-trust units, issuer-issued rights/options/derivatives and listed real-estate CIS units** are EIPs.
- **Debentures are EIPs except ABS and structured notes.** That single carve-out is the most examinable line in the appendix.
- **Immediate currency exchange only.** A spot exchange is an EIP; a forward, swap or option is not.
- **The CIS limb is a restrictive investment-policy test**, permitting deposits, gold, other EIPs, and hedging or efficient portfolio management, with a narrow change-of-law escape.
- **Staples must be of EIPs.** Stapling to a SIP removes the benefit.
- **Trap:** a genuine bond is an EIP, but a structured note dressed as a bond is not, and the firm bears responsibility for the classification even where it is outsourced.
