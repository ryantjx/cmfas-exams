# Chapter 1 — The Capital Markets Industry in Singapore and Participants in the Capital Markets

Who and what makes up the Singapore market: the primary and secondary markets, the institutions that intermediate and regulate them, and the listing framework. The examination tests roles, powers and thresholds — which body may do what, and which applicant fits which listing route.

## Learning Objectives

The candidate should be able to:

- Distinguish the primary and secondary markets and the intermediation each needs.
- Identify each institutional participant and the activity it may lawfully conduct.
- Explain the role, powers and rule-making authority of MAS, SGX, SGX RegCo, SGX-ST, CDP, SGX-DT and SGX-DC.
- Compare Mainboard and Catalist admission requirements.
- Explain listing methods, GDRs, the IPO and introduction routes, and continuing obligations.

## 1.1 Introduction

### 1.1.1 The Capital Markets in Singapore

An important function of the capital markets is to give businesses opportunities to raise capital to fund their activities. That capital-raising takes place in the primary market.

#### 1.1.1.1 The Primary Market

Businesses raise capital by issuing securities — **shares (common stock or equity securities), bonds (fixed-income securities) and company warrants**. Investors supply the capital by buying those instruments.

By buying, investors take on risk, because the price of a financial instrument fluctuates with the company's performance. Changes in value eventually lead investors to sell, either to realise a profit or to remove a poorly performing instrument from the portfolio.

#### 1.1.1.2 The Secondary Market

Trading outside the initial capital-raising activity takes place in the secondary market. The secondary market therefore lets investors **manage or transfer risk** to other parties.

Risk transfer or risk management can also be achieved by trading futures or other derivatives instead of simply selling the shares or bonds — for example futures, options, issuer or company warrants, and leverage certificates. SGX and its subsidiaries provide both capital-raising and risk-management opportunities through their product offerings.

| Exchange | Capital raising | Risk management |
|---|---|---|
| **SGX-ST** | Shares, bonds, company warrants | Issuer warrants, leverage certificates |
| **SGX-DT** | — | Futures, options |

### 1.1.2 Exchanges

Primary and secondary market activity can take place in the **over-the-counter (OTC)** market or on **regulated exchanges**. The OTC market is also called the **"call around" market**, because participants call each other directly to determine interest in buying or selling a given product.

An exchange provides a centralised market where buyers and sellers congregate, allowing efficient discovery of the prices and quantities at which each participant is willing to trade.

### 1.1.3 Financial Intermediaries

Different intermediaries connect businesses that need capital with public investors.

In the **primary market**, raising capital is complex. In equities, new shares are issued through an **Initial Public Offering (IPO)**, in which the business must present accurate information on its financial standing, future potential and other relevant matters. Financial intermediaries — banks or institutions holding a CMS licence to advise on corporate finance — are usually appointed as **issue managers**, because most businesses lack that expertise in-house.

In the **secondary market**, an investor holding a security would struggle to find a buyer directly. **Brokers** act as middle agents, matching selling and buying interest or routing orders to an exchange for matching.

## 1.2 Institutional Participants in the Capital Markets

### 1.2.1 Banks

Different types of bank are licensed under the **Banking Act 1970** and may provide capital markets services such as dealing in capital markets products. Dealing in capital markets products means, whether as principal or agent, making or offering to make with any person, or inducing or attempting to induce any person to enter into or offer to enter into, any agreement for or with a view to acquiring, disposing of, entering into, effecting, arranging, subscribing for or underwriting any capital markets products.

The licence types are Qualifying Full Banks and Full Banks, Wholesale Banks, and Merchant Banks.

#### 1.2.1.1 Qualifying Full Banks and Full Banks

Qualifying Full Banks and Full Banks provide the **whole range of banking business** approved under the Banking Act. They may take deposits of any amount in any currency, including offering savings accounts. They also provide capital markets products, custodial business, underwriting, corporate finance, and in some cases distribute insurance products for insurers.

Because they face no restriction on deposit products, many are also in retail banking. Their client base is more diversified: mass retail, private banking, and accredited and institutional clients.

#### 1.2.1.2 Wholesale Banks

Wholesale Banks provide the full range of banking business but are **restricted in deposit taking**. They may take foreign-currency deposits in any amount but are restricted in Singapore dollars. They may accept SGD **fixed deposits of at least S$250,000**. They may operate SGD savings or current accounts, but such an account opened for a Singapore-resident natural person should **not be interest-bearing** without MAS's prior approval.

Many wholesale banks therefore solicit high-net-worth clients through private banking arms, and those clients are usually more interested in capital markets products for investment.

#### 1.2.1.3 Merchant Banks

Merchant Banks are licensed under **section 55S of the Banking Act** since **1 July 2021**. They may **not accept Singapore deposits or borrow Singapore dollars from the public** in any form, except from banks, finance companies, their shareholders and companies controlled by their shareholders. They do not operate in the retail space but may undertake banking or deposit-taking business under the Banking Act and the **Banking (Merchant Banks) Regulations 2021**.

### 1.2.2 Capital Markets Services Licence Holders

CMS licence holders dealing in capital markets products include broker/dealer companies, corporate finance advisory companies, and providers of custodial services.

**Product financing** — credit for purchases — and **specified-product lending** — lending securities or products under the rules — are different activities and must not be confused.

#### 1.2.2.1 Broker and Dealer Companies

Apart from banks, broker/dealer companies are another class of financial intermediary. Unlike banks, they **do not take deposits**; they specialise in matching buyers with sellers. In the capital markets ecosystem they are also **Trading or Clearing Members**, providing trading or clearing services to customers so those customers can trade on the exchange.

#### 1.2.2.2 Corporate Finance Advisory Companies

Corporate finance advisory companies provide corporate finance advisory services, including acting as **issue managers or sponsors** for listings on the Mainboard or Catalist. They also advise on arrangements, reconstructions and take-overs, acquisitions and disposals.

#### 1.2.2.3 Providers of Custodial Services for Capital Markets Products

These entities must hold a CMS licence for custodial services. Custodians serve institutional and individual clients. Besides general custody, they maintain records of holdings, process corporate actions and support settlement.

#### 1.2.2.4 Others (Non-CMS Licence Holders)

**Finance companies** operate under the Finance Companies Act and may be exempt institutions for specified CMS licensing purposes, subject to their legal and regulatory conditions.

**Remote overseas trading and clearing members** are exempt from a Singapore CMS licence if the member:

- is incorporated outside Singapore;
- is a member of the relevant approved exchange or approved clearing house, or recognised market operator or recognised clearing house;
- does not serve Singapore residents;
- is not a Singapore-regulated financial institution;
- is regulated in its home jurisdiction; and
- has a home regulator with a suitable MAS information-sharing arrangement.

A remote **clearing** member additionally cannot have a MAS-regulated financial-institution affiliate; a remote **trading** member has no such restriction.

## 1.3 The Regulatory Framework and Regulatory Bodies

The framework aims at acceptable risk and financial-system stability, safe and efficient market infrastructure, fair, orderly and transparent markets, and informed customers.

The securities and derivatives markets are regulated by **MAS, SGX, SGX RegCo, SGX-ST, CDP, SGX-DT and SGX-DC**. These bodies originate and issue the rules governing trading and clearing, being:

- the SFA and SFR;
- the SGX-ST Mainboard and Catalist Rules;
- the SGX-ST Rules;
- the CDP Clearing Rules, CDP Settlement Rules and CDP Depository Rules;
- the SGX Futures Trading Rules; and
- the SGX-DC Clearing Rules.

### 1.3.1 Roles of the Regulatory Bodies

#### 1.3.1.1 Monetary Authority of Singapore

MAS was established under the **MAS Act**, in force since **1972**. Its mission is to promote sustained non-inflationary economic growth and a sound and progressive financial centre.

Its four functions are to:

- **i.** act as Singapore's central bank — conducting monetary policy, issuing currency, overseeing payment systems, and serving as banker to and financial agent of the Government;
- **ii.** conduct integrated supervision of financial services and financial-stability surveillance;
- **iii.** manage Singapore's official foreign reserves; and
- **iv.** develop Singapore as an international financial centre.

MAS administers the legislation relevant to the capital markets industry:

- the **SFA**, the main legislation governing the industry;
- the **Securities and Futures (Licensing and Conduct of Business) Regulations (SFR(LCB))**;
- the **Securities and Futures (Financial and Margin Requirements for Holders of Capital Markets Services Licence) Regulations (SFR(FMR))**;
- the **Securities and Futures (Corporate Governance of Approved Exchanges, Approved Clearing Houses and Approved Holding Companies) Regulations**; and
- the **Financial Services and Markets Act 2022**.

The SFA gives MAS wide powers, including to:

- approve exchanges and clearing houses;
- review amendments to the rules and regulations of exchanges and clearing houses;
- take disciplinary action — warning, fine, reprimand, suspension of licence, revocation of licence and issuance of a prohibition order — where a licensed person contravenes a licence condition or restriction, a direction issued by MAS under the SFA, or a provision of the SFA;
- inspect the books of an exchange, a person operating an exempt market, a clearing house, a person operating an exempt clearing facility, a CMS licence holder, an exempt person or a representative; and
- investigate alleged or suspected contravention of any SFA provision or written direction.

#### 1.3.1.2 Singapore Exchange Limited

SGX is Asia's leading market infrastructure, operating equity, fixed-income and derivatives markets to the highest regulatory standards. As Asia's most international multi-asset exchange, it provides listing, trading, clearing, settlement, depository and data services, with a large proportion of listed companies and bonds originating outside Singapore.

SGX is the **world's most liquid offshore market for the benchmark equity indices of China, India, Japan and ASEAN**, and offers commodities and currency derivatives. Headquartered in AAA-rated Singapore, it is recognised for risk management and clearing capability, and offers a fully integrated value chain from trading and clearing to settlement and depository services.

SGX regulation follows **six guiding principles**:

| # | Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Disclosure-based regulation** | Facilitate fair access to information for all market users, achieving a fair, orderly and transparent market. |
| 2 | **Comprehensive risk management** | Focus regulatory attention on the safe and efficient operation of the clearing houses, requiring a comprehensive, integrated and reliable approach to counterparty risk from clearing and trading members and other risks within the clearing houses. |
| 3 | **Risk-based targeting** | A pragmatic, risk-based approach: supervisory activity under principles 1 and 2 is tailored to risk profiles developed for issuer sponsors and member firms, with resources allocated to the highest-risk matters. |
| 4 | **Balanced international best practice** | Rules and regulatory activity consistent with international best practice for exchanges and clearing houses, balancing recognised practice against local needs and conditions. |
| 5 | **Transparency** | Be open and transparent in regulatory operations to the extent consistent with statutory obligations and the public interest. |
| 6 | **Frontline regulator, managing regulatory conflict** | MAS is the statutory regulator with oversight of SGX's regulatory responsibilities; SGX performs the frontline role in maintaining fair, orderly and transparent markets and safe, efficient clearing. |

SGX maintains close collaboration with other regulatory and enforcement agencies — including MAS, the **Commercial Affairs Department (CAD)** of the Singapore Police Force, and the **Accounting and Corporate Regulatory Authority (ACRA)** — on regulatory policy, risk management, oversight and enforcement.

#### 1.3.1.3 Singapore Exchange Securities Trading Limited

SGX-ST is a subsidiary of SGX incorporated under the **Companies Act 1967**. It undertakes day-to-day regulation of the securities market and administers the **SGX-ST Rules**, which govern access to and conduct in the SGX-ST securities market; SGX-ST Members must adhere to them.

SGX-ST is the **only approved securities exchange in Singapore** and sets the rules and the membership and trading requirements, with power to discipline non-compliance. Its rules let companies and investors meet capital-raising and investment objectives — for example the listing requirements for companies wishing to raise capital and have securities traded. Already-listed companies can raise further capital through the market.

Companies choose the **Mainboard** or **Catalist**. The Mainboard caters to more established companies with higher entry and listing requirements such as minimum profit and market capitalisation. Catalist caters to smaller or fast-growing companies under a different model: companies must be brought to list by **approved Sponsors** through an IPO, and there are **no quantitative entry criteria** set by SGX — the Sponsor decides whether the applicant should be listed.

The **SGX-ST Listing Manual** contains the Mainboard Listing Rules and the Catalist Listing Rules and covers:

- **i.** listing requirements;
- **ii.** acquisitions;
- **iii.** realisations;
- **iv.** takeovers;
- **v.** timely disclosure; and related matters.

#### 1.3.1.4 The Central Depository

CDP, established in **1987**, is a wholly owned SGX subsidiary providing integrated clearing, settlement and depository facilities for the securities market, including equities, fixed-income instruments and funds. It principally serves Singapore but has links with other central depositories to support cross-border settlement.

CDP holds book-entry securities deposited with it as **bare trustee** for the collective benefit of depositors. Securities are **immobilised** at CDP and ownership transfers by book entry; physical certificates of immobilised instruments are kept with a CDP-nominated custodian bank.

All trades executed on SGX must settle on **T+2** — two exchange business days after the trade day. Each trade settles on a **gross basis** during an end-of-day settlement run, in which securities move from the seller's account to the buyer's and vice versa. SGX introduced an **Intra-day Settlement Run in 2021** to allow earlier settlement and reduce counterparty risk in the clearing system.

Investors holding direct CDP accounts should ensure their trading accounts with SGX-ST members are **linked to their direct securities accounts** before trading. The linkage is effectively a standing instruction to CDP to act on the member's sole instruction to debit and credit securities for BUY and SELL contracts executed through that trading account.

In **2008** CDP launched the **Pre-Settlement Matching Service (PSMS)** to replace the manual process in which depository agents and SGX-ST members agreed trade details by telephone before manually affirming settlement details in CDP. PSMS brings Singapore securities processing in line with global markets through a **straight-through-processing** environment, automating pre-settlement matching before settlement at CDP. It improves operational efficiency, minimises operational risk by removing manual error and delay, and mitigates settlement failure through early matching of instructions. Depository agents and SGX-ST members upload a data file or key in instructions without prior communication with their settlement counterparties.

Participants may settle **delivery-versus-payment (DVP)** or **free-of-payment (FOP)**. For DVP, CDP acts as **central counterparty** and ensures simultaneous exchange of cash and securities; under FOP, participants make money settlement without involving CDP. The DVP Rules govern delivery-versus-payment settlement through CDP.

CDP Clearing Members must adhere to the **CDP Clearing Rules, CDP Settlement Rules and CDP Depository Rules**.

#### 1.3.1.5 Singapore Exchange Derivatives Trading Limited

SGX-DT, established in **1978**, is a wholly owned SGX subsidiary carrying on the business of establishing, conducting and regulating a futures market whose underlying assets include commodities and financial instruments under the SFA.

SGX-DT lays down rules and requirements for orderly trading and settlement of futures and options on interest rates, equity and equity indices, and energy, covering Asia, Europe and the United States. These are contained in the **Futures Trading Rules**, which govern SGX-DT Trading Members.

#### 1.3.1.6 Singapore Exchange Derivatives Clearing Limited

SGX-DC is a wholly owned SGX subsidiary providing clearing for:

- products listed and traded on SGX-DT; and
- OTC commodity and financial derivatives trades registered for clearing via the **SGX Titan OTC platform**.

SGX-DC is a **Qualifying Central Counterparty (Qualifying CCP)**. Bank members and subsidiaries of a banking group that are SGX-DC Members are subject to **lower capital requirements** for their trade and default-fund exposures under the **Basel III** framework of the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision. As an SGX-DC bank member or participant, one therefore benefits from lower capital cost.

SGX-DC Clearing Members are governed by the **SGX-DC Clearing Rules**.

#### 1.3.1.7 Singapore Exchange Regulation Pte Ltd

In **2017** SGX established **SGX RegCo** as an independent regulatory subsidiary to enhance the governance of SGX as a self-regulatory organisation and to **segregate the exchange's regulatory functions from its commercial and operating activities**. SGX RegCo undertakes all frontline regulatory functions and has a **separate board of directors** from SGX.

## 1.4 SGX Listing Framework

SGX-ST gives companies an avenue to raise capital and sets the rules for that avenue through its listing requirements.

### 1.4.1 Listing Process

A company seeking listing first applies to SGX-ST. Before applying it engages an adviser as **issue manager** to prepare the application — services usually provided by a bank or institution with a CMS licence permitting corporate finance advice. The company then authorises the issue manager to deal with SGX-ST on its behalf.

The issue manager plays an active role: gathering required information, liaising with SGX-ST on the application, and making the final submission. Legal and accounting advisers are engaged as well. The process then runs:

- preparation and due diligence;
- submission of listing documents;
- SGX-ST review, with MAS/SGX prospectus review where a prospectus is involved;
- public exposure;
- registration;
- launch;
- allocation; and
- commencement of trading.

### 1.4.2 Initial Public Offer Timeline

#### 1.4.2.1 Main Board

For the Mainboard, the Section A review commonly takes about **four to six weeks**. Then follows the Section B or prospectus review, any research blackout, **public exposure on MAS OPERA for at least seven calendar days**, registration and launch, and listing.

### 1.4.3 Listing Requirements

#### 1.4.3.1 Methods of Listing

**Primary listing.** The company must meet SGX-ST's initial listing requirements for either a Mainboard or Catalist listing. After listing it must comply with all continuing listing obligations.

**Secondary listing.** A company already listed on an acceptable exchange — the **home exchange** — may seek a secondary listing on SGX-ST **Mainboard without complying with SGX-ST's continuing listing obligations**. Secondary-listed companies must announce to SGX-ST and provide all information and documents to it **at the same time** as they are released to the home exchange, and must comply with all home-exchange rules and the laws of the jurisdiction of incorporation.

#### 1.4.3.2 Global Depositary Receipts

A company already listed overseas must abide by home-exchange rules, and may also list and raise funds via **GDRs on SGX-ST's Mainboard**. GDRs are specialist products offered **only to institutional and accredited investors**. GDR issuers must provide all information and documents, **in English**, to SGX-ST at the same time as they are released to the home exchange.

### 1.4.4 Initial Public Offering or Introduction

A company seeking listing, on a primary or secondary basis, should indicate its preference at the outset and, at application:

- **i. For an IPO** — indicate whether it intends to issue new shares or offer existing shares to the investing public, then lodge a **prospectus with MAS** prepared under the relevant regulations. During the listing process the prospectus is subject to public comment for **up to two weeks**.
- **ii. For an introduction** — no public share offer is made; an introductory document is submitted to SGX-ST and is **not exposed for public comment** in the way a prospectus is.

### 1.4.5 Mainboard Admission Requirements

An IPO applicant generally satisfies one of three quantitative tests:

| Route | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **1. Profit test** | At least **S$30 million** consolidated pre-tax profit for the latest financial year, and at least **three years** of operating track record |
| **2. Market-capitalisation with profit** | Profit in the latest financial year, market capitalisation of at least **S$150 million**, and at least **three years** of operating track record |
| **3. Market-capitalisation with revenue** | Operating revenue in the latest completed financial year and market capitalisation of at least **S$300 million**. REITs and business trusts without historical financials may use this route if they can demonstrate immediate operating revenue. |

The applicant should be financially healthy, with **positive operating cash flow** considered. Debts owed to the group by directors, substantial shareholders and controlled companies should be **settled before listing**.

**Mining, oil and gas issuers** carry special market-capitalisation, reserves, working-capital, independent-qualified-person and independent-director requirements.

Also applying are shareholder-spread, independent-director, promoter-moratorium, prospectus, accounting-standard, domicile, currency and continuing-obligation requirements, including:

- **25% public shareholding** and at least **500 shareholders** for issuers below S$300 million market capitalisation;
- at least **two independent directors**;
- **Singapore-resident independent directors** for foreign issuers; and
- **promoter moratoria** tied to the profit or market-capitalisation route used.

### 1.4.6 Catalist Listing

Catalist permits **only a primary listing** and requires an SGX-authorised **full sponsor**. There is **no minimum quantitative entry test**; the sponsor decides suitability, and SGX-ST may impose conditions, delay or refuse admission. A **continuing sponsor** must be retained throughout the listing.

#### 1.4.6.1 Key Criteria

- **15%** of post-invitation share capital in public hands;
- at least **200 shareholders**;
- at least **two independent directors**; and
- at least **one Singapore-resident independent director** for a foreign issuer.

Mining, oil and gas applicants must substantiate resources and maintain sufficient working capital.

#### 1.4.6.2 Offer Document

Offer documents are lodged on **Catalodge** and follow prospectus-style disclosure, including the **SFA liability provisions**, and are exposed for at least **14 days**.

#### 1.4.6.3 Differences between the Mainboard and Catalist

| | Mainboard | Catalist |
|---|---|---|
| Target issuer | Established companies | Smaller or fast-growing companies |
| Regulation model | Exchange-regulated | **Sponsor-supervised** |
| Quantitative admission test | Yes — profit/market-cap | **None** |
| Listing type | Primary or secondary | **Primary only** |
| Public float | 25% (below S$300m) | **15%** |
| Shareholders | 500 (below S$300m) | **200** |
| Sponsor | Not required | **Full sponsor, and a continuing sponsor** |

### 1.4.7 Mainboard and Catalist Continuing Listing Obligations

Obligations are broadly similar: **material disclosure, periodic reporting, additional share issues, corporate transactions, corporate governance, interested-person transactions and free-float requirements**.

### 1.4.8 Roles and Responsibilities of the Sponsor and SGX-ST regarding Catalist

#### 1.4.8.1 Sponsors' Roles and Responsibilities

Sponsors must remain **eligible**, seek SGX advice on issues affecting market integrity, maintain adequate systems and registered professionals, act **independently and professionally**, maintain regular contact with the issuer, use qualified specialists where necessary, and avoid conflicts.

#### 1.4.8.2 SGX's Role and Responsibilities

SGX reviews sponsor due diligence, continuing activities and breaches, and administers the sponsor regime, including the admission and supervision of registered professionals.

#### 1.4.8.3 SGX's Actions for Non-Compliance on Sponsors and Registered Professionals

SGX may **reprimand**, require **education or rectification**, impose **conditions**, or **suspend** sponsors and registered professionals.

### 1.4.9 Explanation of Terms

**"Listed"** means appearing on SGX-ST's official list. **"Quoted"** may mean trading on an OTC quotation platform without being listed, and without the full listing and continuing-obligation framework.

### 1.4.10 Access to Material Information about Listed Issuers

Listed issuers must disclose material information via **SGXNet**, including information about subsidiaries and associated companies where needed to avoid a false market or to inform investment decisions.

### 1.4.11 Shareholders' Rights to Vote at Meetings

Shareholders receive meeting notices — normally **14 days** for ordinary resolutions and **21 days** for special resolutions — and may attend or appoint proxies. Resolutions are voted by **poll**.

### 1.4.12 SGX's Actions on Breaches whether on Mainboard or Catalist

SGX may **warn**, impose a **composition fine**, require **education or independent reviews**, require **remediation**, **suspend** an issue manager or trading, or **remove** an issuer from the official list.

### 1.4.13 Listing of Debt Securities on SGX-ST

SGX-ST may list fixed- and floating-rate bonds, convertible and exchangeable bonds, covered bonds, asset-backed securities, loan participation notes and hybrid capital securities. Issuer, trustee and trust-deed, offering-document, fee and continuing-obligation requirements apply.

### 1.4.14 Listing of Special Purpose Acquisition Companies on SGX-ST

The SPAC framework created a listing vehicle that raises IPO capital to acquire an operating business or assets through a merger, share exchange or similar combination. At IPO a SPAC has **no operating history and no revenue-generating business**, so information about the target develops only when a proposed business combination is announced.

## Exam cues

- **Mainboard vs Catalist:** S$30m profit; or profit with S$150m market cap; or revenue with S$300m market cap — against **no quantitative test** at Catalist. Catalist is **primary listing only** and always needs a **continuing sponsor**.
- **Numbers that decide questions:** **T+2** settlement; **25%** public float (Mainboard below S$300m) and **15%** (Catalist); **500** vs **200** shareholders; **14/21 days** ordinary/special notice; OPERA exposure at least **7 days** (Mainboard) and **14 days** (Catalist).
- **Bank licence limits:** Full Banks take deposits of any amount and currency; Wholesale Banks need **S$250,000** minimum SGD fixed deposits and must not pay interest on SGD savings/current accounts of Singapore-resident natural persons without MAS approval; Merchant Banks **cannot** take SGD deposits or borrow SGD from the public except from banks, finance companies, shareholders and shareholder-controlled companies.
- **Regulatory architecture:** MAS is the statutory regulator; SGX-ST is the **only approved securities exchange**; CDP is **bare trustee** holding **immobilised** book-entry securities; SGX-DC is a **Qualifying CCP** giving Basel III capital relief; **SGX RegCo (2017)** has a separate board and takes all frontline regulation.
- **SGX's six guiding principles:** disclosure-based regulation, comprehensive risk management, risk-based targeting, balanced international best practice, transparency, and frontline regulator managing regulatory conflict.
- **Trap:** a secondary listing **does not** take on SGX-ST continuing obligations, but must announce to SGX-ST **at the same time** as to its home exchange. GDRs are **institutional and accredited only**, and disclosure must be **in English**.
