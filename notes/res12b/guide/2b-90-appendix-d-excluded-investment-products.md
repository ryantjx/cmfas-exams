# Appendix D — Excluded Investment Products

**Excluded Investment Products (EIPs)** fall outside the Specified Investment Product regime, so neither the CAR nor the CKA is required. The full list of categories in the Schedule to the Securities and Futures (Capital Markets Products) Regulations 2018 is reproduced in **[Appendix E of Part I](../chapter-1b-appendix-e-excluded-investment-products/)**. This page states only what a derivatives dealer needs beyond that list.

## What differs in Part II

Most derivatives are **not** EIPs, which is why the EIP list matters less on a derivatives desk than on a securities desk. The distinction that decides the question is **listed versus unlisted**, and the EIP carve-outs that survive into derivatives are narrow:

- **Exchange-traded derivatives** are generally **SIPs**, not EIPs, even where the underlying is an EIP. An EIP underlying does not make the derivative an EIP.
- **Debentures remain the sharp exception.** Debentures are EIPs **except asset-backed securities and structured notes** — the carve-out that catches structured notes sold to a derivatives client.
- **Units in a listed real-estate CIS** are EIPs; an OTC derivative over that CIS is not.
- **A structured deposit** is not automatically outside the SIP regime; classification depends on the structure and the disclosure given.

## Exam cues

- **EIP status does not flow through to the derivative.** The derivative is assessed on its own terms.
- **Debentures:** EIP except **ABS and structured notes** — the most examinable line in the appendix.
- **Trap:** a client's existing EIP holdings tell you nothing about whether the derivative on the same underlying needs the CAR or CKA.
