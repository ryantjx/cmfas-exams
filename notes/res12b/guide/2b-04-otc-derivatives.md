# OTC derivatives

**Learning objectives.** The candidate should be able to:

- understand what over-the-counter (OTC) derivatives are;
- describe the regulatory regime for dealing in OTC derivatives;
- understand general practices for dealing in OTC derivatives.

## 4.1 Overview of over-the-counter derivatives

OTC derivatives are typically traded through a **decentralised network** — for example via a dealer — as opposed to being traded on a central exchange such as **Singapore Exchange Derivatives Trading Ltd (SGX-DT)**, **Intercontinental Exchange Futures Singapore Pte Ltd (IFSG)** or **Asia Pacific Exchange Pte Ltd (APEX)**.

Two consequences follow from the absence of an exchange:

- Unlike on a regulated exchange, the **price of an OTC derivative is not necessarily publicly published** for an OTC trade.
- The risks relating to OTC derivatives are generally viewed as **opaquer** than those of exchange-traded contracts, because of the **bilateral counterparty risk** within these trades — a counterparty may default before the contract expires and fail to make the required payments.

### 4.1.0 How counterparty risk is mitigated

OTC derivatives can be **sent to clearing houses for clearing and settlement**. The clearing house then acts as a **central counterparty (CCP)** to the trade — it interposes itself as buyer to the seller and seller to the buyer. In the event of a default, the buyer or seller faces the **clearing house's credit risk**, which is backed by **safe amounts of margin and a mutualised default fund**.

**Singapore Exchange Derivatives Clearing Ltd (SGX-DC)** provides CCP services for OTC commodity and OTC financial derivatives trades for a global participant base.

### 4.1.1 Common OTC derivatives contracts

The named instruments are **interest rate swaps (IRS)**, **non-deliverable interest rate swaps (NDIRS)**, **cross-currency swaps (CCS)**, **forward rate agreements (FRA)** and **commodity swaps**.

| Instrument | Description |
|---|---|
| **Interest Rate Swaps (IRS)** | An agreement between two counterparties in which a stream of future interest payments is swapped for another, based on a specified principal amount. |
| **Non-Deliverable Interest Rate Swaps (NDIRS)** | A currency swap between major and minor currencies that is not convertible. In an NDIRS there is **no physical exchange of currency flows**, unlike an ordinary currency swap. |
| **Cross-Currency Swaps (CCS)** | An agreement between two counterparties to swap interest payments **and principal** on loans denominated in two different currencies. |
| **Forward Rate Agreement (FRA)** | An OTC contract between two counterparties that determines the **rate of interest, or currency exchange rate, to be paid or received on an obligation beginning at a future start date**. |
| **Commodity Swaps** | An agreement between two counterparties to exchange cash flows dependent on the **price of an underlying commodity** — commonly used as a hedge against swings in commodity prices. |

Examples of OTC products cleared by SGX-DC:

- **IRS — SGD.**
- **NDIRS — THB, MYR.**
- **Commodity swaps — iron ore, rubber, freight forward agreements, coking coal, petrochemicals, oil, gas.**

### 4.1.2 Trade repositories

In **2013** MAS implemented regulatory regimes for **OTC derivatives clearing facilities and trade repositories**. **DTCC Data Repository (Singapore) Pte Ltd** established the first local trade repository and was approved by MAS as a **licensed trade repository**. It enables reporting of OTC derivatives trades and is an important market infrastructure for improving **regulatory oversight and transparency** of the Singapore OTC derivatives market.

### 4.1.3 Differences from the exchange-traded securities market

There are several differences between the traditional securities market traded on an exchange and the OTC derivatives market, including the roles intermediaries play.

- **i. Clientele.** Unlike the securities market, the OTC derivatives market is populated with **more sophisticated and institutional players**, with little participation from retail traders.
- **ii. Relationship with clients.** In a purchase of a share or futures contract, the obligations between an intermediary and a client are **largely discharged after the trade is completed** and positions are closed out. For OTC derivatives the intermediary's obligations with its counterparty **may last for several years**, depending on the length of the contract.
- **iii. Standardisation.** Securities markets are largely **standardised**, with regulations and procedures supporting trading and clearing facilities. The OTC derivatives market is **not centrally cleared** and consists mostly of **privately negotiated** transactions between two counterparties, and the contract terms may not always be **documented properly and wholly**.
- **iv. Brokering.** A broker on a traditional exchange earns commissions by **bringing together buyers and sellers** licensed to trade within the exchange. **Inter-dealer brokers** facilitate trades **between major dealers** buying and selling OTC derivatives products.

## 4.2 Regulatory regime for dealing in OTC derivatives

In the wake of the **Global Financial Crisis in 2008**, MAS improved regulation of the OTC derivatives market to meet the objectives set by the **G20 and the Financial Stability Board**. The SFA has seen several changes:

- **i.** Introducing a new regulatory regime for **trade repositories**.
- **ii.** **Extending** the regulatory regimes for market operators, clearing facilities and capital markets intermediaries **to the OTC derivatives markets**.
- **iii.** **Mandating central clearing and reporting** of certain OTC derivatives contracts.

Intermediaries dealing in OTC derivatives contracts are required to hold a **CMS licence** to deal in capital markets products.

### 4.2.0 The reporting regime

The SFA and the **Securities and Futures (Reporting of Derivatives Contracts) Regulations** ("SFR(RDC)") provide for a reporting regime for specified derivatives contracts. Under **Part 6A of the SFA** on Reporting of Derivatives Contracts, a **"specified person"** who is a party to a specified derivatives contract must report prescribed information to either a **licensed local trade repository** or a **licensed foreign trade repository**.

**Specified persons** include:

- banks;
- bank subsidiaries;
- merchant banks;
- finance companies;
- insurers;
- **CMS licence holders**.

The following derivatives contracts, **provided they are traded or booked in Singapore**, are prescribed under **Regulation 5 of the SFR(RDC)** as specified derivatives contracts for the reporting regime:

- **interest rate** derivatives contracts;
- **credit** derivatives contracts;
- **foreign exchange** derivatives contracts;
- **commodity** derivatives contracts;
- **equity** derivatives contracts.

### 4.2.1 Clearing of OTC derivatives contracts

**Section 129C of the SFA** states that every specified person who is a party to a **specified derivatives contract** must **clear** such contracts with an **approved or recognised clearing house**.

"Specified derivatives contract" means any derivatives contract that is, or belongs to a class that is, **prescribed by MAS under the Securities and Futures (Clearing of Derivatives Contracts) Regulations**.

### 4.2.2 Reporting of information

The information that must be reported, as stated in the **First Schedule to the SFR(RDC)**, includes information pertaining to:

- the **contract**;
- the **counterparty**;
- **clearing**;
- other **transactional data**.

## 4.3 SGX-DC clearing rules for OTC derivatives

The main type of OTC derivative that SGX-DC clears is **OTC commodity derivatives** — for example iron ore swaps and rubber swaps.

OTC commodity derivatives are covered by the definition of **"Non-Relevant Market Contracts"** under the SGX-DC Clearing Rules.

### 4.3.1 General requirements

- OTC commodity contracts are registered through the **Titan OTC** system.
- Clearing members must **post margin** on contracts at rates prescribed by SGX-DC.
- Clearing members must **procure initial margin from their customers** and **ensure customers comply with maintenance margin** requirements.
- **Variation margin** may need to be paid to SGX-DC to account for **mark-to-market differences** in the contracts.
- The **same requirements** apply to clearing members' **proprietary trades**.
- Members may at their discretion set **higher margin rates or requirements** than the Clearing House rules.
- Members must **review their internal rates and requirements on a continual basis** to ensure compliance.

## 4.4 Risk disclosure

CMS licence holders dealing in **futures contracts**, **OTC derivatives contracts with currency or a currency index as underlying**, or **leveraged FX contracts**, must furnish a **risk disclosure document** to clients **before the setting up of their accounts** (Regulation 47E of the SFR(LCB) — Risk Disclosure by Certain Persons).

CMS licence holders must also furnish a **risk warning statement** to clients and **obtain their acknowledgement** before executing trades in **overseas-listed investment products** (MAS Notice on Sale of Investment Products, SFA 04-N12).

It is essential that investors and counterparties **fully understand the risks** of the product they are dealing with. Where MAS does not intend to prescribe the form of risk disclosure for all OTC derivative contracts, CMS licensees may rely on **established industry standards**. For instance, the **ISDA Dodd-Frank Disclosure documents** can be used if the firm has assessed that they **meet the MAS risk disclosure requirements**. Examples of MAS risk disclosure statements are **Form 13** and **Form 14**.

## 4.5 General OTC derivatives dealing practices

There is **no standardised procedure** for dealers and brokers to conduct OTC derivatives trades. However, the **Singapore FX Market Committee** has provided guidance on the conduct of dealing in OTC derivatives, in **Chapter IX (OTC Derivatives Dealing Practices) of "The Singapore Guide to Conduct and Market Practices for the Wholesale Financial Markets"** — also known as **"The Blue Book"**.

### 4.5.1 Dealing procedures

- **All material terms must be stated clearly**, with **no ambiguity** over those terms.
- Unless otherwise provided by an applicable broker agreement, the **principal should specify to the broker** where a transaction involves **less than the minimum dealing amount**.
- The information must be given **early to the broker** where a principal is **unable to deal with another principal on the ground of an insufficient credit limit**.
- Principals, brokers and other relevant parties should follow an **established confirmation format and terminology** to reduce misunderstanding.

### 4.5.2 Standard documentation

One available format is the **ISDA Master Agreement** (or similar templates developed by industry bodies such as the **Futures Industry Association**). The ISDA Master Agreement outlines standard terms to be applied to a derivatives transaction between two parties. Among other things, these terms include provisions relating to:

- **governing law**;
- **conditions precedent**;
- **netting**;
- **undertakings**;
- **events of default**;
- **termination**.

The Master Agreement is **open for negotiation** between the parties and its terms can be **customised** to suit their circumstances.

The Master Agreement can be supplemented with **credit support documents**, which deal with the **treatment of collateral provided between the parties**. This may be appropriate where:

- transactions involve parties with **very different credit ratings**, giving rise to significant counterparty risk; or
- **collateral is provided to cover potential exposure**.

Parties should pay particular attention to the **governing law** employed in the credit support documents — **English, New York or Japanese law** — because **the manner in which collateral is transferred may differ significantly** between them.

### 4.5.3 Settlement of differences

Where a **broker is unable to substantiate its quotation** and a difference is payable to the principal, the broker should **close the OTC derivatives product at the next available price** and **settle the difference with the principal** — for example by issuing a cheque for that amount.

## Exam cues

- **Reporting versus clearing are different obligations.** Reporting under **Part 6A of the SFA** and the SFR(RDC) goes to a **trade repository**; clearing under **section 129C** goes to an **approved or recognised clearing house**.
- **"Specified person" is a closed list** — banks, bank subsidiaries, merchant banks, finance companies, insurers and CMS licence holders. Retail clients are not specified persons.
- **Five prescribed asset classes report**: interest rate, credit, foreign exchange, commodity and equity derivatives — and only if **traded or booked in Singapore**.
- **The reporting content is set by the First Schedule to the SFR(RDC)**: contract, counterparty, clearing and other transactional data.
- **SGX-DC clears OTC commodity derivatives** as **"Non-Relevant Market Contracts"** through **Titan OTC**; financial OTC derivatives are within SGX-DC's CCP service but the clearing rules in §4.3 address commodities.
- **Members may set margin above the Clearing House minimum** but must **review continually** — the discretion is not a licence to under-margin.
- **Form 13 is the derivatives risk disclosure**; **Form 14 is the overseas-listed product risk warning**. The account-opening disclosure (Reg 47E) applies to futures, currency/currency-index OTC derivatives and leveraged FX.
- **The Blue Book** (Singapore Guide to Conduct and Market Practices for the Wholesale Financial Markets), Chapter IX, is the source of OTC dealing practice guidance — not a statute.
- **Six ISDA Master Agreement heads of term** are examinable: governing law, conditions precedent, netting, undertakings, events of default, termination.
- **Governing law in the credit support document matters** because collateral transfer mechanics differ — a favourite distractor.
- **Trap:** a CCP **reduces** bilateral counterparty risk but **replaces it with clearing-house credit risk**, and adds margin calls and default-fund contributions. It does not eliminate risk.
- **Trap:** the broker that cannot substantiate a quotation must **close at the next available price and settle the difference** — not simply cancel the trade.
