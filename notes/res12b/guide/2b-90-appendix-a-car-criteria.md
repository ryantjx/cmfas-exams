# Appendix A — Criteria for the Assessment of a Customer Account Review

The **Customer Account Review (CAR)** applies to **listed** Specified Investment Products. The full four assessment routes, the qualification tables and the consequences of failing the CAR are set out once, in **[Appendix B of Part I](../chapter-1b-appendix-b-car-criteria/)**; this page states only what differs for a derivatives dealer.

## What differs in Part II

- **The products are the same listed SIPs.** A listed structured warrant, daily leveraged certificate, listed CFD or exchange-traded derivative is a listed SIP, so the CAR applies to the account before the first transaction, exactly as it does for listed securities.
- **The CKA applies to unlisted SIPs.** Most OTC derivatives a Part II dealer transacts — non-listed CFDs, OTC structured notes, knock-out and binary structures sold off-exchange — are **unlisted** SIPs, so the relevant gate is the **CKA**, not the CAR.
- **Both can be needed for one client.** A client with one account trading both listed and unlisted SIPs must satisfy the CAR for the listed leg and the CKA for the unlisted leg; satisfying one does not satisfy the other.
- **The exchange-traded/OTC split is the deciding distinction,** not the asset class. The same underlying — an equity index, a currency pair or a commodity — can be a listed SIP in futures form and an unlisted SIP as a bilateral swap.

## Exam cues

- **Listed → CAR; unlisted → CKA.** That single mapping answers most questions in this area.
- **A derivatives dealer needs both,** because the Part II product set spans listed and unlisted SIPs.
- **Trap:** passing the CAR does not permit dealing in an unlisted SIP, and the CKA does not substitute for the CAR on a listed product.
