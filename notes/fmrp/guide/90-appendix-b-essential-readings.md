# Appendix B — Essential Readings

The source list runs to **twenty-three items** across **five publishers**. Use the current version of each, and check whether the title, notice number or web location has changed. The full list follows, grouped by publisher as in the study guide.

| 3 | **Securities and Futures (Financial & Margin Requirements for Holders of Capital Markets Services Licences) Regulations** |

| 5 | **Income Tax Act** |
| 6 | **Deposit Insurance and Policy Owners' Protection Schemes Act** |
| 7 | **Corruption, Drug Trafficking and Other Serious Crime (Confiscation of Benefits) Act** |
| 8 | **Mutual Assistance in Criminal Matters Act** |
| 9 | **Terrorism (Suppression of Financing) Act** |

These nine statutes supply the statutory backbone. The **SFA** carries licensing, conduct and market misconduct; the two sets of SFA regulations carry business conduct and the financial and margin requirements. The **Banking Act, section 47** governs banking secrecy and the permitted disclosures in Part I and Part II of the Third Schedule. The **Income Tax Act** and the **Deposit Insurance and Policy Owners' Protection Schemes Act** sit alongside the tax and depositor-protection material. The **CDSA**, **MACMA** and **TSOFA** operate as a package for financial crime.

## Singapore Foreign Exchange Market Committee (`http://www.sfemc.org/`)

| # | Instrument |
|---|---|
| 10 | **The Singapore Guide to Conduct & Market Practices for the Wholesale Financial Markets ("the Blue Book")** |

The Blue Book is the source of the **general dealing principles and market conduct**, the ethics and behavioural standards, governance and risk management principles, back office practices, and the dealing practices for individual instruments including **FX, debt securities, swaps and OTC derivatives**. It also supplies the **market disruption** material examined in Chapter 7.

## Global Foreign Exchange Committee (`https://www.globalfxc.org/fx_global_code.htm`)

| # | Instrument |
|---|---|
| 11 | **FX Global Code** |

The FX Global Code supplies the **six leading principles** — ethics, governance, information sharing, execution, risk management and compliance, and confirmation and settlement — examined through Chapters 2 to 6.

Items 12 and 13 underpin the fit-and-proper and misconduct-reporting obligations; item 14 is the **minimum entry and examination** notice, which is the counterpart to the competency requirements in RES 1B Chapter 2. Item 15 supports the Government securities conduct material.

Items 16 and 17 are the **CMI** and **bank** AML/CFT notices respectively — the CMI notice applies directly, the bank notice is included for comparison. Item 18 sits behind the appointment due-diligence file.

**Items 19 to 21 are a matched trio on SGD lending to non-resident market participants**: one notice for **banks** (MAS 757), one for **merchant banks** (MAS 1105), and one for **CMS licence holders** (SFA 04-N04). Any question on the restriction must be read against the notice that governs the particular institution type — the same commercial transaction can fall under a different instrument depending on the entity.

Item 22 is the **SGS market rules and practices**, which is the source for the Government securities conduct requirements examined in Chapter 5.

## How to read the list efficiently

**Read the notice, not the Act, for operational detail.** The SFA creates the offences and the licensing gate, but the *conduct* requirements a representative applies day to day — confidentiality, order handling, confirmations and settlement — are in the **SFR(LCB)** (item 2), the **Blue Book** (item 10) and the **FX Global Code** (item 11).

**Read the guideline when the question is about discretion.** Fit and proper (item 12) is a judgment-based test, so the guideline's factors matter more than any single rule.

**Match the notice to the institution type.** The SGD lending restriction (items 19–21) is the clearest example: three notices, three institution types, one substantive rule.

**Treat the AML/CFT notices as a pair with a difference.** Item 16 applies to CMIs; item 17 is the bank equivalent. Knowing which applies is examinable.

**Do not neglect the peripheral statutes.** The **Deposit Insurance and Policy Owners' Protection Schemes Act** (item 6) and the **Income Tax Act** (item 5) appear peripheral but support questions on depositor protection and on the permitted disclosures that override confidentiality.

## Exam cues

- **Item 12 (FSG-01) is the fit-and-proper source**, not item 2. Confusing the guideline with the regulation is a common error.
- **Item 14 (04-N09) is the minimum entry and examination notice**; it is a different instrument from item 13 (04-N11), which covers **misconduct reporting**. The numbers are easy to transpose.
- **Items 19, 20 and 21 are three separate SGD-lending notices** — MAS 757 for banks, MAS 1105 for merchant banks, SFA 04-N04 for CMS licence holders. A question naming the institution determines the answer.
- **Item 16 (SFA04-N02) applies to CMIs; item 17 (Notice 626) applies to banks.** The study guide includes both so that the CMI regime can be read against the bank standard.
- **Item 22 is not on the MAS site** — the SGS rules and market practices sit on `sgs.gov.sg`, which is a useful discriminator in a question about publication source.
- **Item 23 is published by ABS, not MAS**, and is the only item covering benchmark rate setting.
- **Items 10 and 11 are different documents from different bodies**: the Blue Book is **SFEMC**, Singapore-specific and covers wholesale markets broadly (FX, debt securities, swaps, OTC derivatives); the FX Global Code is **BIS/GFXC**, global and FX-specific with **six principles**.
- **Item 4 is cited by section, not by whole Act** — section 47 and Parts I and II of the Third Schedule are the operative provisions on banking secrecy.
