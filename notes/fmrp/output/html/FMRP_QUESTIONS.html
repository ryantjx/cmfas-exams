Chapter 4 - Governance, Risk Management and Compliance

14. [Source reference | Single choice] [FMRP §4.4] Which one of the following statements relating to segregation of duties is TRUE?

  • a. Back office staff should not have any communication with front office personnel.
  • b. It is acceptable for front office, middle office and back office to share access to their respective systems.
  • c. Management should establish appropriate information barriers between trading, risk management and processing units.
  • d. Market Participants should not share any trading profit and loss reports with the middle office.

Answer and explanation

Answer: C. Management should establish appropriate information barriers between trading, risk management and processing units.

Rationale: Information barriers and separated system access reduce the risk that one function can originate, alter and conceal a transaction. Segregation does not mean zero communication.

Answer and explanation

Answer: D. Front office personnel should not have access rights to middle office systems to process trades.

Rationale: Restricting front-office access to middle-office systems is a direct control over unauthorised processing and concealment. Physical proximity or communication alone is not the key control.

Chapter 5 - Execution and Handling of Orders

Answer: A. An IDB must accurately relay the special conditions to the counterparties.

Answer: B. Engage in taking its own positions by executing its own trades.

Rationale: An IDB should intermediate rather than take its own proprietary positions in the transaction. The other functions can be part of proper intermediation.

Rationale: The six principles are ethics, governance, information sharing, execution, risk management and compliance, and confirmation and settlement. Profit maximisation is not a principle; the Code is about conduct, not commercial outcome.

Chapter 2 — Ethics, Behavioural Standards and Professional Conduct

326. [Original | Single choice] [FMRP §2] A client offers a dealing representative an expensive watch shortly after a large trade is executed. The most appropriate response is to:

  • c. Accept it and share it with the desk
  • d. Accept it and report it at the next annual attestation

Answer and explanation

Answer: B. Decline it in line with the firm’s gifts and entertainment policy and record the offer

Rationale: A gift offered soon after a trade creates at least the appearance of inducement. Policy compliance plus a record deals with both the substantive and the evidential risk.

  • d. An unrelated friend followed on social media

Answer and explanation

Answer: A, B, C

Rationale: Connected persons are defined by close family and household relationship. A social media connection does not create the relationship the rules contemplate.

328. [Original | Single choice] [FMRP §2] A representative wishes to deal for a personal account. Which control is fundamental?

  • a. Dealing only in securities the firm does not cover
  • b. Informing a colleague on the desk before dealing
  • c. Pre-clearance from compliance and a prohibition on dealing ahead of a client order
  • d. Dealing only through an external broker

Answer and explanation

Answer: C. Pre-clearance from compliance and a prohibition on dealing ahead of a client order

Rationale: Pre-clearance plus a front-running prohibition addresses both the conflict and the misuse of client order information. Dealing through an external broker does not remove either risk.

329. [Original | Multiple response] [FMRP §2] When must a Market Participant submit a misconduct report to MAS in respect of a representative?

Answer and explanation

Answer: A, B

Rationale: The reporting duty attaches to misconduct and to failures of fitness and propriety. A motoring fine and an internal expense breach are employment matters, not reportable misconduct.

Answer: D. Only for the purposes for which the client has provided it

Rationale: Client money is held for the client’s purpose. Internal approval and short-term repayment do not create a right to use it, and using it as proprietary margin is a plain misapplication.

Answer: A. A leveraged foreign exchange trading account

Rationale: The disclosure attaches to leveraged products where the client can lose more than the amount invested. Cash and deposit products do not carry that exposure.

Chapter 4 — Governance, Risk Management and Compliance

345. [Original | Single choice] [FMRP §4] Which is the most effective control against a rogue trader concealing losses?

  • a. Sharing system access across front, middle and back office
  • b. Requiring dealers to take annual leave of at least one continuous week
  • c. Requiring front office staff to reconcile their own positions daily
  • d. Allowing the dealer to settle their own trades for speed

Answer and explanation

Answer: B. Requiring dealers to take annual leave of at least one continuous week

Rationale: Mandatory consecutive leave is a recognised detective control precisely because concealment requires continuous presence. Letting a dealer settle their own trades removes the separation that caught Barings.

346. [Original | Multiple response] [FMRP §4] Which are elements of an effective segregation of duties?

  • a. Information barriers between trading, risk management and processing
  • b. Front office having no access rights to process trades in middle office systems
  • c. The same person approving and reconciling a trade
  • d. Independent reporting lines for risk and compliance

Answer and explanation

Answer: A, B, D

Rationale: Approval and reconciliation by the same person defeats the purpose of the control. The other three are standard elements of segregation.

347. [Original | Single choice] [FMRP §4] A customer fails to meet a margin call. The action that best minimises loss is to:

  • a. Extend further credit to allow time to recover
  • b. Wait for the customer to make contact
  • c. Close out the customer’s positions once the margin call remains unanswered within the agreed time
  • d. Open an offsetting position for the customer

Answer and explanation

Answer: C. Close out the customer’s positions once the margin call remains unanswered within the agreed time

Rationale: Enforcing the close-out is the contractual remedy and caps the exposure. Extending credit increases it; offsetting without instruction creates a new position.

348. [Original | Single choice] [FMRP §4] Which is the most important objective of business continuity planning for a Market Participant?

  • a. Ensuring staff can work from home
  • b. Ensuring all payments are withheld during a disruption
  • c. Ensuring front office can keep dealing
  • d. Ensuring treasury operations across front, middle and back office can continue

Answer and explanation

Answer: D. Ensuring treasury operations across front, middle and back office can continue

Rationale: Continuity has to cover the whole processing chain, because a front office that can trade but cannot settle is worse than one that pauses. Withholding all payments contradicts the prohibition on unilateral suspension.

349. [Original | Multiple response] [FMRP §4] Which controls are appropriate for off-premises dealing?

  • a. Recording the trade details promptly and completely
  • b. Reporting the trade through approved channels so the counterparty receives it promptly
  • c. Relying on the dealer’s personal mobile phone records as the sole record
  • d. Including the trade in the normal trade capture process

Answer and explanation

Answer: A, B, D

Rationale: Off-premises dealing raises the risk of unrecorded or late-booked trades, so the trade must enter the same capture process and reach the counterparty promptly. A personal device as the only record fails record-keeping requirements.

350. [Original | Multiple response] [FMRP §4] Which statements about technology and risk management are correct?

  • a. Sufficient capacity should be maintained for normal and peak loads
  • b. Data confidentiality should be preserved by encrypting messages
  • c. Representatives should have unrestricted access to all systems
  • d. System changes should be tested before deployment

Answer and explanation

Answer: A, B, D

Rationale: Unrestricted access across systems defeats segregation of duties. Capacity planning, encryption and pre-deployment testing are standard technology controls.

351. [Original | Single choice] [FMRP §4] Before executing an OTC derivative trade governed by a Master Agreement, a Market Participant should obtain its own legal, tax and accounting advice in order to:

352. [Original | Multiple response] [FMRP §4] Which are appropriate when appointing an interdealer broker?

Chapter 5 — Execution and Handling of Orders

356. [Original | Single choice] [FMRP §5] An IDB receives an order from a principal subject to special conditions. The IDB must:

  • c. Charge more brokerage for the extra work
  • d. Relay the special conditions accurately to the counterparty

Answer and explanation

Answer: D. Relay the special conditions accurately to the counterparty

Rationale: The IDB is a conduit. It must convey the order as given; altering or dropping conditions misrepresents the principal’s instruction.

357. [Original | Single choice] [FMRP §5] Which action must an IDB NOT take?

  • b. Pass settlement instructions to the counterparties
  • c. Reveal counterparty names once the deal is concluded
  • d. Relay price quotations on receipt of an order

Answer and explanation

Answer: A. Take its own position by executing its own trades

Rationale: An IDB acts as intermediary, not principal. Taking its own position puts it on the other side of its clients’ trades and destroys the neutrality the role depends on.

358. [Original | Single choice] [FMRP §5] A price maker’s quotation is dealt simultaneously by several IDBs. Subject to its limit, the price maker must:

  • a. Honour only the first IDB to deal
  • b. Honour the quotation with each IDB, up to its limit
  • c. Choose which IDB to honour
  • d. Split the amount equally among the IDBs

Answer and explanation

Answer: B. Honour the quotation with each IDB, up to its limit

Rationale: A firm quotation is a commitment, so the price maker must honour it with each IDB subject to its limit. Choosing counterparties or splitting equally would breach the firmness of the quote.

  • d. Obtain the approval of the customer’s board

Answer and explanation

Answer: C. Obtain the express approval of her own senior management

Rationale: An off-market rate transfers value and requires internal senior approval, which also creates the record that the rate was deliberate and authorised.

Answer: D. Indicate the minimum amount that can be done

Rationale: Stating what can be done lets the counterparty decide with accurate information. Committing without limit, or executing first, creates an unauthorised exposure.

Answer: A, B, C

381. [Original | Single choice] [FMRP §7] A party considers suspending settlement unilaterally because its own systems have failed. It should:

Answer: B. Not suspend unilaterally, because failed settlements create credit exposure and can cascade into systemic risk

Rationale: For SGD settlement the guidance is specific: follow MAS instructions, which take precedence over general SFEMC recommendations.
